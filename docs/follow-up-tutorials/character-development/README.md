# The Wraith's Three Trials

**Character development storyboard · Proposal · Solo · 30–40 minutes · Ten core frames**

[Illustrated storyboard](storyboard.html) · [Collection](../README.md) · [Shared contract](../design-contract.md)

At **Echo Court**, the Wraith shows three travelers caught at the same broken crossing: a porter unable to carry the supplies, a keeper unable to call help and a guard unable to hold a crowded lane. The player borrows a possible future for each trial, changes it through real character controls and sees the consequences before making choices on the permanent character.

**The Wraith:** “I can lend you a possibility. What you keep must be your own choice.”

Three small lanterns fill as the travelers reach the refuge. The Wraith's reset is openly a practice service, not a promise of free ordinary respecialization. The player never has to buy a real perk or spend a permanent pickpoint to finish.

## Access and four-gate map

Offer when unspent pickpoints first become relevant, with a repeat invitation from the Wraith. Borrowed profiles have a declared budget of earned points and practice gold, calculated from the actual chosen attribute, nexus and perk costs. Keep the offered budget visible; do not hard-code a number before selecting catalog tiers. Restore all base values, spent points, perks, toggles, gear-derived effects, XP and buffs on exit.

Propose **112 × 112 tiles**. Echo Court (56,56) holds the Wraith and three lanterns. North (56,87) is Weight Walk, East (87,56) the Keeper's Pen, South (56,25) the Guard's Court and West (25,56) the Unwritten Road.

| Gate | Composition | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| North · Weight Walk | Supply stacks and a short safe carry route | Attributes and observable derived capacity |
| East · Keeper's Pen | Summon recipe and open spawn space | Nexus access versus skill progression |
| South · Guard's Court | Single and multiple-opponent lanes | Perk costs, conditions and tradeoffs |
| West · Unwritten Road | A different supply problem and threat arrangement | Choose a build under a finite budget |

Gates open in sequence but each trial can be replayed from the refuge. A displayed build card is a proposed summary of real server state, not an alternative character-allocation system.

## Ten storyboard frames

### 01 · What belongs to you — 3 minutes

**Picture:** The player's real Statistics face a translucent practice ledger. Three unlit lanterns sit behind the Wraith.

**Voice:** “Look at the points you have. You will leave with those choices still yours.”

**Play:** Open Statistics and Perks, inspect available pickpoints and one unaffordable next tier, then enter the separate practice profile. Compare the real and borrowed budget labels. Open a purchase confirmation and cancel it before any mutation.

**Learn:** Available points, already spent points, gold and requirements are distinct; opening a confirmation does not spend anything.

**Evidence:** Correct profile snapshot, actual confirmation cancellation and unchanged practice values after cancel. Merely reading a guide paragraph is not purchase literacy.

**Recovery / observe:** Players with no earned real points can still enter. Can the tester find the actual cost and cancel without believing they changed the permanent character?

### 02 · A pack has a limit — 4 minutes

**Picture:** A porter waits beside supplies whose load is slightly above the borrowed character's capacity. A safe drop-off lies nearby.

**Voice:** “One journey or two. First see what the pack can hold.”

**Play:** Attempt to take the proposed order, read the capacity rejection, then inspect the directly purchased **Carry** attribute and carrying capacity. Spend one practice point on Carry through the real Statistics confirmation. The current model gives 20 capacity per Carry point. Take a quantity that now fits and deliver it to the porter.

**Learn:** Attribute choices have measurable effects outside combat. Taking two trips remains valid ordinary play; this trial specifically demonstrates a capacity change once.

**Evidence:** Genuine rejected load, confirmed point spend, authoritative capacity increase and successful pickup within the new limit.

**Recovery / observe:** Tune the stack against the actual capacity so one Carry purchase makes it fit. Do not teach the retired Physique/Coordination cross-attribute formula; those names remain only for legacy-save migration. Never falsify the derived statistic to fit the scene.

### 03 · Access is not expertise — 4 minutes

**Picture:** In East Pen, the Stag row names Animal Nexus as its blocker. A second card shows Summoning as a separate skill.

**Voice:** “Permission to try is not practice at succeeding.”

**Play:** Inspect the current Stag recipe with sufficient borrowed Summoning but insufficient Animal Nexus. Spend one practice nexus point through ordinary controls, then perform the real summon. Compare the stable nexus value with skill XP after the action.

**Learn:** Nexus unlocks eligibility; using a skill earns its XP. Spending on nexus does not grant arbitrary skill levels.

**Evidence:** One genuine nexus restriction, accepted purchase and server-owned summon. Capture nexus and XP separately.

**Recovery / observe:** A failed mix still demonstrates an attempt, but the spawn demonstration needs a successful retry. Supply only the outstanding allowance. Let players inspect a different nexus without making them buy all six.

### 04 · Practice leaves a different mark — 3 minutes

**Picture:** A small safe workstation and sparring lane share a readable Statistics view. XP feedback rises beside the chosen activity.

**Voice:** “Do the work once. Watch which number answers.”

**Play:** Choose a short craft or combat task already familiar to the player. Observe its actual skill and Overall XP change, then inspect earned and available pickpoints. If a level threshold is demonstrated, author the practice XP just below that real threshold and disclose the starting state.

**Learn:** Skill experience, Overall progress and available pickpoints have related but distinct roles; not every successful action earns a point immediately.

**Evidence:** Server XP event and correct subsequent Statistics. No invented level-up when the threshold was not crossed.

**Recovery / observe:** A capped or unrewarded action must be replaced with a valid short task. In replay, place the XP farther from the threshold and see whether the tester expects an automatic point after every action.

### 05 · Buy an effect you can use — 4 minutes

**Picture:** South Court offers a single attacker and a later pair. The perk catalog stays readable from the refuge.

**Voice:** “Read what changes, what it costs, and when it applies.”

**Play:** Inspect a currently visible combat perk with a deterministic measurable modifier, such as the appropriate catalog entry for multi-combat or a weapon arrangement. Read tier cost, gold, conflicts and conditions. Purchase its first tier, equip a compatible kit and run the matching encounter.

**Learn:** A perk's effect depends on its actual conditions; buying a named benefit does not improve every situation.

**Evidence:** Confirmed purchase, correct active condition and authoritative effective modifier during ordinary play. Do not infer a perk's strength from one lucky damage roll.

**Recovery / observe:** Select the exact offered perk/tier during implementation from the visible catalog; hidden legacy entries are not public recommendations. A mismatched weapon gets a clear reason and a safe kit change.

### 06 · A higher tier has a price — 3 minutes

**Picture:** Two possible next purchases sit beside the remaining budget. One would leave insufficient points for the other.

**Voice:** “The second choice uses what the first one leaves.”

**Play:** Inspect the next purchasable tier and compare it with saving points for a different requirement. Attempt an unaffordable or conflicting purchase in practice, read the real rejection, then choose an affordable option or deliberately keep the points. Run a brief activity that uses the resulting build.

**Learn:** Tiers, conflicts and a finite budget create opportunity costs. Saving points is a legitimate choice.

**Evidence:** Genuine blocker and a valid budget state followed by useful play. No compulsory maximum-tier purchase.

**Recovery / observe:** The Wraith can restore the disclosed trial baseline. Distinguish this reset from ordinary removal stones. Ask the tester to show the first cost they would have to meet, not to memorize every tier price.

### 07 · What is worn, what is owned — 3 minutes

**Picture:** A practice gear rack offers an item with a registered perk effect beside an otherwise comparable item. The owned-tier display remains visible.

**Voice:** “Some strength comes from the thing you wear. Take it off and look again.”

**Play:** Equip and remove a catalog-valid item that contributes a perk or modifier. Compare owned tier with effective effect, then restore the appropriate kit. If the selected perk is toggleable only at its maximum tier, inspect that restriction; actual toggling is an optional extension using a qualified borrowed profile.

**Learn:** Purchased ownership, temporary enablement and gear-derived effects are different states. Unequipping an item is not a refund.

**Evidence:** Exact gear instance change and expected effective modifier delta while purchased tiers and point budget remain unchanged.

**Recovery / observe:** Validate a real native item/affix combination first. If no visible example exists, register the practice exemplar explicitly. Do not invent a universal perk-toggle button or imply every perk can be switched off.

### 08 · Reconsider without guessing — 3 minutes

**Picture:** The Wraith lays a clearly labeled practice removal stone beside the earlier purchase ledger.

**Voice:** “Outside this court, changing your mind has its own requirements.”

**Play:** Inspect a registered removal item appropriate to a purchased practice attribute, nexus or perk. Use it through ordinary item handling, observe the actual refund/removal and inspect a dependent action that may now be blocked. Only then use the Wraith's disclosed practice reset to prepare the final trial.

**Learn:** Real removal is item-specific and validated; a tutorial reset is not a public free respec service.

**Evidence:** Real item consumption and matching ownership/point delta. Refund quantity must come from the selected removal rule, not an assumed universal one-point refund.

**Recovery / observe:** Supply one compatible practice stone and preserve its transaction ID. Wrong-item or minimum-attribute rejections must be safe. Verify the player does not confuse a toggle, unequip and removal.

### 09 · Choose a future for the crossing — 6 minutes

**Picture:** West Road combines a supply load, an optional summon route and a manageable guard encounter. Its arrangement differs from all three trials.

**Voice:** “These people need a way through. Spend only what your plan needs.”

**Play:** Start with a fresh declared practice budget. Inspect the task and select an attribute/nexus/perk combination, then execute the plan through actual pickup, summoning, crafting or combat. Change two variables between attempts: load threshold and threat arrangement. Provide at least two affordable validated routes.

**Learn:** Build around an intended activity and constraints instead of copying a prescribed allocation.

**Evidence:** Budget-valid real purchases and successful traveler/supply delivery. Accept spare points, multiple trips and alternate tactics; the player must apply at least one chosen change in the resulting action.

**Recovery / observe:** Preview costs before commitment and offer an explicit practice reset after a failed plan. Record why the tester chose a change and whether its actual effect solved the intended problem.

### 10 · Leave the choice unwritten — 2 minutes

**Picture:** The three lanterns glow. The practice ledger closes, leaving the real Statistics and unchanged unspent points.

**Voice:** “You have seen several roads. You do not owe me a choice today.”

**Play:** Restore the permanent profile, inspect one real next purchase and open then cancel its confirmation if the player wants to rehearse. Record a proposed next goal in the tutorial journal without spending. Leave or replay a trial.

**Learn:** The player owns the eventual commitment; borrowed progress did not purchase their real build.

**Evidence:** Exact restoration of base values, earned/spent points, perk tiers/toggles, gear and XP; completion once. Any optional real purchase occurs outside tutorial requirements.

**Recovery / observe:** Handle departure during an open confirmation by clearing the practice request. Later ask the player to find the cost and condition for their intended activity; a deliberate decision to save points passes.

| Gear-derived versus purchased tier | 07 | Equip/remove changes effect without refund |
| Maximum-tier toggles | Reflection B · quiet strength | Use a real toggleable visible perk at qualified tier; test that lower-tier and non-toggleable entries refuse |
| Negative perks and drawbacks | Reflection C · the costly bargain | Only if currently offered: borrow one drawback, observe its actual consequence and removal. No core recommendation to take it |
| Removal stones versus practice reset | 08 | Exact item-specific removal and restored eligibility |
| Finite-budget specialization and saving | 09–10 | Successful alternate build, no mandatory real spend |

Reflections take 5–7 minutes each. Coverage means understanding each category and trying representative changes, not buying every perk or nexus. Catalog prices and visibility are authoritative; archived perks are not offered simply because code still recognizes them.

## Implementation dependencies and audit

New work includes Echo Court, three trial NPCs, profile budget snapshots, exact source/effective comparison display, practice-only reset, evidence and proposed journal goal note. Reuse real Statistics/Perks confirmation and server spend/removal paths. The active item catalog currently contains no removal stones even though removal handlers exist: core 08 requires a registered practice item with a handler-recognized name, native icon and normal item-use route. This content registration is a dependency, not an existing item the novice can already buy. The current character model has twelve directly purchased attributes; the old six-attribute system must not appear as the lesson's allocation model.

Audit no points, exact remaining budget, insufficient gold, attribute/nexus maximum, tier ceiling, conflicts, hidden perk, toggle below maximum tier, gear removal during an active effect, removal stone at minimum base value, item-instance replacement, two clicks on confirmation, reconnect after spend and early exit with a prompt open. Verify that restoring a practice build does not erase permanent guild/god state or a preexisting buff. Test all final-route budgets against actual costs.

## Human playtest emphasis

Use the shared protocol. Target 5/6 distinguishing skill practice from nexus access through an action, and 4/6 solving the final task with a valid self-chosen allocation. At least 5/6 must show cancellation and correctly identify the remaining real budget after departure. Do not grade a specific build as correct when several routes work. A later task asks players to diagnose one real requirement and choose whether to spend, save or change the plan.
