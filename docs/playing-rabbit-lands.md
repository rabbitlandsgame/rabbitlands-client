# Playing Rabbit Lands

## Seasons and the clock

Each season lasts one real calendar month. Winter, spring, summer and autumn repeat every four months, giving three full seasonal years in one real year. Seasons change at midnight UTC on the first of the month, shared by everyone.

| Season | Real months |
| --- | --- |
| Winter | January, May, September |
| Spring | February, June, October |
| Summer | March, July, November |
| Autumn | April, August, December |

Spring favours showers, summer favours clearer skies and warmer sunlight, autumn brings more fog and rain with amber light, and winter brings cooler light and more snow in temperate and mountain climates. Local climates still matter: deserts, tropical regions and volcanic lands keep their characteristic weather.

Winter coats exposed ground and tree crowns with snow. Spring brings fresh greens, summer deeper greens, and autumn turns deciduous foliage gold, orange and rust red. Evergreens retain their green foliage. Tropical, desert and volcanic regions keep their local character. Snow cover follows the season and clears when winter ends.

Use **#day** or click the clock to see the current season and when the next one begins, alongside the existing day or date information. The six-hour day/night cycle continues independently. Seasonal scenery works in all three appearance modes.

## Companions

Open Summoning and choose **Behaviour** to direct your summons at any level, including level zero. The six choices control which targets a companion may attack. New companions assist against your opponent; a saved passive choice stays passive. Directions do not bypass a recipe's skill, Animal Nexus, ingredients, ether or food requirements.

Companions follow their owner through ordinary walkable routes. Their health decays over time, so plan supplies and replacements. A small companion normally lasts about five minutes without fighting or healing.

## Dispatches and crafting

The quest journal lets you track a dispatch again after stopping its guide. Stopping tracking keeps your progress. A destination on another map names that map in the guide.

For the medicine errand, gather the Sage, Hearthroot and Bramble in one trip, in any order. You can make both medicine bottles together; keep the spare for the second delivery. Issued supplies that do not fit in your pack go into storage, and the guide identifies them.

The crafting filter **Has ingredients only** checks ingredients. You may still need food, ether, a tool, knowledge or a higher skill. The recipe details explain those requirements, and an unsuccessful attempt shows a red result.

The small **i** button beside the mixing skill selector shows recipes with ingredients for at least one mix in your pack; it lights green when active. **A/L** switches the recipe list between alphabetical names and required level, low to high; level order shows each recipe's level. Hover for the controls' meanings. These choices are remembered across sessions. Storage counts are unchecked here, so i is disabled while mixing from storage. Shorter item labels such as **Iron Sword** appear in mixing and inventory; the original name still works in searches. Long selected names shrink to fit without enlarging the window, and the full name is available on hover.

Choose a mixing design in **Settings → HUD → Mixing design**, or open **Mixing → ••• → Mixing design**. The five presets are **Compact workbench**, **Tabbed fieldbook**, **Narrow edge dock**, **Quick-mix popover**, and **Shallow mixing tray**. **Customize mixing…** opens the live editor: drag sections, resize their corners, or change positioning, text, colors and borders. Undo/Redo, section resets, per-preset edits and five named custom designs work independently of your inventory and main HUD. Save keeps edits; Cancel or Escape restores the previous design.

The **Recipes**, **Mix** and **Queue** tabs share the fieldbook's footprint. The quick-mix card can be pinned; an unpinned card closes on a world click. Expand **Queue** to manage batches, or use **•••** for ingredient filtering, full requirements and clearing the queue. **Inventory / Storage** selects the ingredient source; storage counts stay unknown here, and the server checks the supplies. A missing tool offers **Take from storage** when a keeper is within reach.

## Equipment and carrying

Choose an inventory design in **Settings → HUD** or **Settings → Inventory**, or open the inventory's **••• → Inventory design** menu. The five defaults are **Unified compact**, **Tabbed satchel**, **Edge dock**, **Adaptive inspect**, and **Shallow bottom tray**. **Unified compact** is the starting design for new players. Inventory designs are saved separately from your main HUD layout.

Choose **Edit inventory…** to arrange the backpack, equipment, text preview and inspection. Drag sections to move them, drag a selected corner to resize, or adjust columns, slot sizes, positioning, colors, opacity and borders in the editor. **Undo**, **Redo**, **Reset this section**, and **Restore default** help refine a design. **Save** keeps your edits; **Cancel** or Escape discards them. Each default remembers its own edits, and you can save up to five named custom inventory designs.

The pack always has 36 server positions and eight equipment positions. Reflowing a grid changes how they are displayed. The search control dims nonmatching slots while keeping their positions stable. The short preview sits below the pack; **Look** or its arrow opens full details in the selected design. A popover can be pinned while browsing. The **Qty** field and **All** set the amount for picking up, depositing, withdrawing and dropping; extra quantity shortcuts, bulk actions and protection options remain in **•••**.

A **Basic Quiver** supplies unlimited ordinary bow ammunition. A **Basic Bolt Case** does the same for a crossbow. Equip the matching container as ammunition. These basic containers add no special accuracy or damage. Ordinary Arrow and Bolt stacks, and special ammunition, still run out. The ranged dispatch supplies a Basic Quiver; quartermasters sell both containers.

Ranged attacks need at least two tiles of space. Start with a nearby Rabbit two to four tiles away: shorter shots are easier. Click the target again to stop aiming, or switch to melee if it reaches you.

At the default Carry attribute of 4, carrying capacity is 160 EMU through overall level 20, 280 at level 50, 480 at level 100, and 680 at level 200. Overall growth slows after level 100. Existing Carry investments still add 20 EMU per attribute point. Completing a tutorial never removes carrying capacity.

In storage, **Store All** moves eligible items together. **Protection…** chooses the slots it should leave alone.

The **Exchange** menu icon or **Ctrl+Shift+E** opens prices from anywhere. **My listings** includes your own listings and listings owned by your original gauntlet parties. Use **Edit price…** for your own items or **Propose price…** for a party vote; **Party votes…** opens that listing's saved party ledger. Buying, posting new listings, cancelling listings and collecting escrow require an Exchange NPC within four tiles. The window updates its available actions as you move.

The **Storage preview** chest icon or **Ctrl+Shift+S** opens a view-only copy of your personal storage. You can browse categories, inspect goods and use the usual sorting and filters. Transfers and favorite-list collection are disabled. Open normal storage through a nearby keeper or chest to transfer items. Both shortcuts can be changed in **Settings → Controls** and are saved with your other hotkeys.

## Fighting and gauntlets

Landed hits and dodges share a refund limit of two action points per combat round. Technique costs and ordinary timed recovery still apply.

Gauntlet death keeps your carried items and returns you at full health at a safe return point. Wave messages name the completed wave; the enemy count distinguishes present enemies from later reinforcement pulses.

Creature drops are held in the party's loot ledger until the run ends. Rare finds are announced to the party. The leader can split common stacks equally, choose recipients, or edit individual quantities, then send every share directly to personal storage. Successful runs add each participant's reserved cache reward. Closing the ledger keeps the items reserved; reopen it with **Gauntlet loot**, the reward cache, or `#gauntletloot`.

Use **Settings → HUD → Edit HUD…** to move **Gauntlet loot** and **Rare drop announcements** independently. Both appear as previews while editing, even outside a run. Choose **Done & save** to keep their positions with the current HUD preset.

Shared rares can have an optional gold value per item. Each buyer approves their payment and must have the full amount in storage before delivery. The party splits that payment equally, including the buyer. **Sell… → NPC buyback** stages a sale of unwanted shared drops at the server's standard value; its gold is also shared equally. Review the payment and sale previews before confirming. NPC sales, payments, and item delivery happen together. Whole-coin remainders follow the run's roster order. Earned creature loot remains reserved if a run ends early; completion caches require a successful clear.

**Sell… → List on party exchange** stages a listing at your selected gold price per item. You can plan it remotely; visit an Exchange NPC within four tiles to confirm distribution and post it. Each sale pays an equal share directly into the original players' storage, including offline players. Partial purchases keep coin rounding fair across the whole listing. Party membership changes do not change who owns the items or receives the proceeds. Reopen **Gauntlet loot → Party exchange** to review listings, proceeds and votes; the run selector includes earlier ledgers.

Any original member can **Propose price…** or **Request withdrawal**. Price proposals show the current and proposed Gold Coins per item. Vote **Yes**, **No** or **Abstain**, at different times if needed. A No blocks the proposal, while abstentions do not count against it. If everyone responds with no No and at least one Yes, the proposal completes immediately. Otherwise, after 14 days, unanswered votes are ignored and the remaining votes decide. Votes may be changed until the request resolves. Each listing allows one open proposal at a time. Approved price changes apply only to unsold items; earlier sales keep their original proceeds. Approved withdrawals wait for an original member to use **Collect approved withdrawal** near an Exchange NPC. Collection puts the unsold items back in shared escrow for the original team. Listings stay available to buyers at the current price during voting and never expire automatically.

## Riding and the interface

The Moor Pony, Golden Plains Horse, Crystalback Tortoise, Giant Meadow Rabbit and Jadefeather Raptor all travel at 1.5 times ordinary running speed. Press Space to gallop at twice running speed: with the normal five-metre-per-second run, those mounted paces are 7.5 and 10 metres per second. Fast mode lasts eight seconds of movement and takes eight seconds to recover. The on-foot walk/run switch does not change mounted speed. Press R to mount or dismount, and use the Mounts window to switch mounts.

The horse-head icon beside Sit on the bottom HUD opens the Mounts window. Its compact grid shows the current collection together, and the search field filters mounts by name. Select a card, then Ride to call or switch mounts.

Right-click your character or your name and status bars to open Banner display. Character and Mount tabs keep the options together, and changes apply immediately and are saved. Right-clicking the mount status line opens the Mount tab directly. Escape or a click outside closes the panel. The full Banners page in Settings remains available.

Trot / gallop status and available gallop time appear directly below your character's resource bars while mounted. Right-click that status line to adjust its display. You can ride in outdoor areas and gauntlets, including gated and trail routes. Riding continues across supported maps and ends when entering a restricted map. Gauntlet gates and encounter requirements still apply while mounted.

The quick spell bar supports a horizontal layout and twelve visible slots. Its orientation and position are saved with your interface layout. Spell and technique wheels leave the character visible in the centre and use translucent backgrounds.
