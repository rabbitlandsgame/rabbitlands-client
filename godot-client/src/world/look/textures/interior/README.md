# Interior surfaces and decals (Landfall Town)

Floor and wall tiles and one-off decals for the building interiors of Landfall Town (map `landfall_town`; spec section 5). The room-shell builder maps the tiles in metres at the repeat below and lays the decals on flat quads. All files are RGB PNG with no alpha; sizes are multiples of 4.

Made 2026-10-27 for the owner's request "Implement your plan" (Landfall town interiors). Generated sources, prompts, attempts and 3x3 tiling checks are kept in the client's ignored stage `.local/asset-authoring/workspace/work-output/landfall-town/` (`surfaces_manifest.jsonl`, `textures/raw/`, `textures/check/`, `decals/raw/`, `tools/`).

### How the tiles were made

Each generated source is a 1254x1254 RGB albedo from the OpenAI image generation tool through Codex (`gpt-image-bridge`), prompted in the structure of `src/dev/map_authoring_pilot/style/texture_packs/*/manifest.json`. Two approved tiles were attached as style references only: `../flagstone-paving.png` and the pack's `weathered-limestone-masonry` tile (oak planks and timber band: the pack's `marine-timber` tile; cobalt tile: the Landfall townhouse concept for the roof-glaze blue). The shared prompt tail:

> Style/medium: quiet painterly hand-painted low-poly fantasy surface in the same brushwork, saturation and scale as Image 1 and Image 2 (style references only; do not copy their content); chunky medium-scale detail that reads at 1-4 m from a game camera, never fine noise. Composition/framing: flat orthographic straight-on square tile filling the whole image, evenly distributed detail with no centre feature; left/right and top/bottom edges must wrap seamlessly with no framing or edge treatment. Lighting/mood: uniform neutral diffuse albedo only. Constraints: both-axis seamless repeat; texture only; diffuse base colour only; no baked normal, height or specular cues. Avoid: edge frame, borders, vignette, gradients, cast shadows, highlights, directional lighting, perspective, horizon, props, text, logos, watermark, photorealism.

The subjects (the full prompt is the subject followed by the tail):

- `oak_planks`: Use case: interior floor. Asset type: square seamless albedo tile. Primary request: an indoor floor of worn wide warm-oak floorboards in a harbour-town house. Materials/textures: boards 20-28 cm wide all running vertically in the image, about ten boards across the tile; staggered butt joints at random lengths of 1.2-2.4 m; honey, amber and mid-brown boards with gentle board-to-board colour variation; thin dark joints; a few small dark iron nail heads at board ends; soft foot-polished lighter areas in broad irregular patches; faint broad grain, no big knots or focal marks. Intended world repeat 2.4 m. Colour palette: warm oak browns, never grey or orange.

- `limestone_flagstone`: Use case: clean indoor floor of a keep and shops. Asset type: square seamless albedo tile. Primary request: large dressed pale cream limestone flagstones laid in a random rectangular (opus romanum) pattern. Materials/textures: square and oblong slabs 40-90 cm, straight sawn edges with softly worn arrises, thin tight light warm-grey mortar joints; slab-to-slab variation of warm cream, sand and pale grey; smooth foot-worn tops with faint mineral mottling. No moss, grass, soil, cracks or rubble. Intended world repeat 3.2 m.

- `cobalt_tile`: Use case: magic-shop and keep floor. Asset type: square seamless albedo tile. Primary request: hand-made glazed ceramic floor tiles in cream and cobalt blue, laid in a perfectly regular square grid of exactly 8 by 8 tiles filling the image edge to edge, grout lines straight and evenly spaced. Materials/textures: square tiles about 25 cm; tiles alternate in a checkerboard between plain cream-glazed tiles and cobalt-blue glazed tiles that carry one simple bold cream four-petal flower motif in the centre; thin pale grey grout; gentle hand-made variation of glaze tone from tile to tile; matte painted glaze, no shine. Image 2 shows the town's cobalt-blue roof glaze colour: match that blue. Intended world repeat 2.0 m.

- `lime_plaster`: Use case: interior walls of houses, shops and the tavern. Asset type: square seamless albedo tile. Primary request: a cream lime-washed plaster wall. Materials/textures: soft hand-trowelled plaster with broad gentle undulations; low-contrast mottles of warm cream, ivory and faint ochre grouped in 0.5-1.5 m clouds; a few short hairline cracks; two or three small worn spots where pale stone shows through. Very low contrast overall, no bricks, no stone courses, no streaks or drips. Intended world repeat 3.0 m.

- `soot_brick`: Use case: smithy walls and hearth surrounds. Asset type: square seamless albedo tile. Primary request: a wall of soot-darkened fired brick in level courses. Materials/textures: chunky bricks about 30 cm long and 10 cm tall in running bond, exactly 20 courses top to bottom, each course offset by half a brick; dark red-brown, umber and charcoal bricks; thick dark grey mortar; broad soft soot smudges spread evenly over the whole tile; a few chipped corners. No vertical streaks, no gradient from top to bottom. Intended world repeat 2.4 m.

- `timber_band`: Use case: horizontal beams, dado rails and the trim band along interior walls. Asset type: square seamless albedo tile. Primary request: the side face of a dark seasoned oak beam, the wood filling the entire image. Materials/textures: grain running horizontally across the whole tile; dark brown and warm umber oak with broad adze-dressed facets; a few dark wooden pegs; no knot holes, no board joints, no edges or corners of the beam visible. Intended world repeat 2.0 m along the beam.

They were made seamless with `.agents/skills/rabbitlands-map-visual-quality/scripts/make_seamless.py` (see the Processing column). Regular patterns were first cropped to a whole number of their own periods (`tools/process_tiles.py` in the stage measures the period from the autocorrelation of the joint profile), so courses and grout lines meet across the wrap. Every tile was checked tiled 3x3.

Known residue: the quilting cut leaves a few short dangling joints in `limestone_flagstone` and two short bricks in `soot_brick`; at 1-4 m they read as irregular laying, not as a seam.

## Import

Nothing in the game loads these files from `res://`. They are inputs of `build_town_interiors.py`, which reads them from disk (`surface_file()`) and embeds them in `landfall_town/client/world.glb`, then moves that package's images to the shared image pool (`shared_images.externalise`) as `build_underground.py` does. The `.gdignore` beside this README keeps the folder out of Godot's import, so it gets no `.import` files and is not exported into the PCK a second time (`export_filter="all_resources"` would otherwise ship about 26 MB of unused PNGs). Should a runtime script ever load one of them from `res://`, remove the `.gdignore` and import them VRAM-compressed with mipmaps: all 25 together are about 17.4 Mpx, about 89 MiB of GPU memory as lossless RGBA8 with mips, against about 11 MiB as BC1.
