# Offline fitting and validation notes

## What can be baked

For rest vertex `x`, joint bind/pose matrices `M_j(t)`, weights `w_j` and pre-skin morph displacement `d(t)`, the target surface is:

`y(t) = sum_j w_j M_j(t) [x + d(t), 1]`.

One fixed bake replaces `x + d(t)` with a single fitted `x'`. It reproduces all poses only if the corrected target lies in the fixed skin's trajectory family. It cannot generally reproduce pose-dependent contact, volume or normal corrections. Solve the constant rest displacement by least squares across representative poses; evaluate held-out poses rather than baking whichever frame looks best.

For a mesh/weight fit, alternate constrained per-vertex weight solves and rest-position solves. Weight constraints are nonnegative, sum to one and limited to supported influences and anatomically plausible local joints. Start from the original geometry plus a good weight-only solution; starting from a large static displacement can settle into a poor local solution. Keep mirrored anatomy coherent, record changed support and verify all coincident seams. A robust p-norm or iteratively reweighted objective can reduce extreme errors; report the corresponding average-error tradeoff and rest-pose deviation.

With fixed weights, a fixed normal can be fit by minimizing projected angular residuals through the blended skin matrices. Validate actual unit output directions. Neither fitted normals nor source normal maps guarantee unchanged tangent-space shading after rest positions or weights change. Inspect actual Forward+ renders, including feet, folds, thin triangles and inside/outside transitions.

## Residual representation

Given a new blended affine skin matrix `[C'(t), b'(t)]`, a residual position field is `inverse(C'(t)) * (y(t) - b'(t)) - x'`. Check conditioning; singular fits require another rig/weight choice. Reconstruct at the actual delivery knots and validate between knots and during blends: the inverse skin operation is nonlinear, so matching knots alone does not imply matching transitions.

Position bases may be arbitrary vectors. **Godot normalizes normal morph endpoints.** Arbitrary PCA normal vectors lose their intended magnitude on import. Use valid unit-normal endpoint poses, preserve a suitable original normal basis, or explicitly prove another representation through the real loader. Normalizing every reconstructed key pose can itself change interpolation near cancellation, even when key-pose directions match.

RenderingServer may ignore morph weights with `abs(weight) < 0.0001`; include that behavior in native error estimates. Account for native u16 weight quantization and packed normals. Use actual joint-palette indices, bind matrices and engine world bone transforms; node indices, skeleton bone indices and skin bind indices are not interchangeable.

A reduced basis can shrink a 30 Hz cache without changing the skin. Run this as a control: structural reweighting may improve the uncorrected surface yet give no additional storage saving once residual corrections are retained. Do not force a skin edit when the unchanged-skin control is better.

## Accepted tortoise precedent

`docs/qa/creature-storage/crystalback-tortoise-corrective-repair.json` binds the exact candidates and acceptance. The corrected 30 Hz reference had 577 basis shapes (221 position, 356 normal), occupying 23.08 MB of its 25.69 MB raw file. Coincident vertices already shared weights; seam welding was not the solution.

The original-weight fixed bake left about 98 mm maximum deviation. Joint least-squares mesh/weight fitting improved that to about 39 mm. Robust fitting produced the visually accepted zero-shape V2 (`53ae0f505843d9835224452ee860a662b2ed0ecbcad659654af966ca28412938`): 1,558,840 raw bytes, 21 bones and four influences. Across the same 4,827 current-client states, body RMS improved from 11.45 to 1.56 mm and maximum error from 97.78 to 33.51 mm. Shading still differed. The user accepted this **specific** compromise and explicitly requested installation and raw creature delivery. Those error values are not general automatic promotion thresholds.

The separate unchanged-skin 328-shape control was closer (roughly 0.103 mm sampled maximum) but larger (15.27 MB raw). This illustrates the visual-quality/storage tradeoff and why a small residual cache or extra joint controls may suit a different actor better.

## Practical validation

- Separate original-to-30-Hz error from 30-Hz-to-bake error. Keep the complete clip set, including retained special clips.
- Sample independent shifted times, full cycles, short and long crossfades at several source phases, default/reset, action interruption and held ends. Report maximum errors by clip and anatomical region in rendered units and normalized by actor height.
- Compare old/new candidates at the same native bone states when only rest geometry/weights differ; this avoids conflating different probe grids. Check original bone matrices remain equal before reusing them.
- A source GLB SHA or `.import` file alone does not prove current editor imports. Genuine editor QA should bind the generated `.scn`, source/destination freshness metadata and actual ResourceLoader route. Raw production loading is a distinct valid test route.
- Keep source landmarks, authoring fits, physical observations, visual acceptance and installed selection as separate records. Reissue candidate witnesses for the final hash without rewriting the original observations to erase differences.
- Corrective buffer bytes are shared mesh data. First-actor CPU allocations include scene/library caches; neither number is automatically per creature instance or total VRAM. Record added surfaces/helper joints and preserve effects, mounts, attachments and grounding behavior.

## Local repair lessons

Check rendered volume as well as local triangle areas and whole-body RMS. Restoring the source base/skin can recover tiny collapsed facets while leaving the overall limb thin; compare rotation-invariant section widths against rest through every clip. Do not change an attack into the opposite arm's counterbalance motion merely because the surface becomes better conditioned. Preserve measured strike height, hand path and downstroke timing unless the requested repair deliberately changes them.

For a stone or armored section that needs volume support, fit a proper rigid rotation **and translation** about the actual mesh-section centre. Direct ownership by an off-axis old bone can create a hanging lump. Bake small support-control curves offline with neutral defaults, exact held endpoints and the delivery cadence; keep original anatomical curves intact. A support field faded into old erroneous weights can still leave an arm-owned calf cap, so validate anatomical ownership at the boundaries as well as the rigid core. Restrict discontinuity checks by actual surface landmarks: nearest-joint distance and existing dominant weights can both miss a root-owned foot corner.

A hierarchy of local quaternion curves cannot always be replaced by a world-space quaternion curve at the same cadence. The composed rotation can wind through a different arc between samples: the Golem arm investigation found a helper that matched every 30 Hz key but differed by almost 180 degrees between keys, despite healthy unit-scale source transforms. Where appropriate, inherit the anatomical parent's continuous rotation and animate a relative offset. Check parent scales and rest/bind conversions independently. Matching rotations also does not align joint pivots: independent section-centre paths can separate a shared elbow or wrist. Validate shared physical anchor positions, the complete shoulder/arm/wrist surface and between-key poses; rigid-core width preservation alone does not certify an articulated arm.

For hard stone or armor, a complete anatomical face partition with separately capped pieces can preserve shape better than fading weights across a collar. Establish actual mesh adjacency and simple, disjoint joint loops; a coordinate flood or an isolated crown patch can miss the connected limb or leak into the torso. Report topology per connected piece: an aggregate Euler characteristic can combine a fist ring and a separate closed torso. If caps add vertices, retain an explicit mapping for every original vertex and separately validate all added geometry; do not weaken an equal-count checker to label unmatched vertices equivalent. Prove native packed boundary coefficients, every piece's face lengths/areas and all changed helper dependencies, including vertices whose raw skin did not change.

Closed rigid pieces with coincident joint centres can still open conspicuous gaps at their rotating rims. Inspect exact worst opening poses as well as sample keys. A physical pivot chain can also change the rendered hand path substantially while preserving every original anatomical animation channel. Measure the actual fist surface, orientation and low-point phase against the authored reference and the last reviewed draft; clip-duration equality is not impact-timing equality. Keep that repair an explicit visual alternative rather than calling it a small cosmetic or exact motion change. See the [Golem rigid-arm draft](../../../../docs/qa/creature-storage/mossbound-stone-golem-rigid-arm-repair.md) for the measured tradeoff.

Cap-centre ownership needs a separate anatomical check. After separating coincident corners into moving pieces, an arbitrary representative from a global weld group can belong to the opposite side. Averaging those rows can attach a torso cap's centre to the arm even when every boundary pair closes correctly. Select representatives from that cap's own faces, or use its explicit body-side anchor; verify the centre trajectory as well as boundary coefficients. A small rounded socket and a rounded generated cap can improve an exposed stone joint while preserving all existing arm curves. Keep original surface vertices separate from rewritten generated-cap ordinals in the correspondence proof. The [shoulder follow-up](../../../../docs/qa/creature-storage/mossbound-stone-golem-shoulder-repair-v7.md) records this narrower repair.

An identity wrapper can change native skeleton paths, order and ancestor counts while preserving the original glTF node prefix and poses. Supply a complete vertex correspondence, compare original bone worlds by unambiguous names and measure the actual engine bone count rather than equating it with the skin palette. Keep raw-field changes distinct from conservatively selected fitting rows. Frame review from source-rendered geometry when preserved gameplay bounds include old outliers; camera changes must not alter actor placement. The Golem example and its partial-repair limits are recorded in `docs/qa/creature-storage/mossbound-stone-golem-ground-pound-repair.md`.

Rigid ownership cannot restore an already distorted fitted rest contour. For separate mane strands or rigid panels, compare restoring the original default contour and normals before assigning whole-piece ownership to existing anatomical joints. Preserve protected face/body components and inspect both sides in PBR. A skin-only fit or helper with lower aggregate error can still produce a worse knee kink or contour; reject that option when rendered motion is worse. Keep the partial improvement and remaining gaps explicit.

When a thumb or other part remains tethered to a neighboring limb, inspect triangle connectivity as well as weights. A real contact bridge needs a localized surface separation and closure; repainting weights alone cannot disconnect its faces. Preserve complete vertex correspondence, winding and nondegenerate surfaces. Check cap boundaries through native packed skin coefficients, not just coincident rest positions. After separation, inspect the adjacent palm/wrist ownership too: a rigid thumb beside palm vertices still influenced by the leg can create a new fin. A local whole-fist repair with a wrist taper can be better than a tiny isolated thumb patch while retaining the same rig and storage.

Compare runtime costs against the active installed model as well as the selected authoring draft. A repair may add zero bones to its selected draft while replacing a much smaller older installed rig. Report both palettes, influence widths, curve counts and raw sizes. Benchmark animation advance and bone updates separately from rendering; do not convert these component timings into whole-game FPS or first-actor cached allocations into per-instance memory. The second comment-repair batch documents these distinctions in `docs/qa/creature-storage/comment-repair-batch-02.md`.
