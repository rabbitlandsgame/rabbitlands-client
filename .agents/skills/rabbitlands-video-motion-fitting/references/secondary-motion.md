# Attachments, back and tail motion

Separate attachment/deformation errors from animated joint motion before damping the whole actor. The wolf pilot's collar shells had foreleg influences and its upper-back fur had leg/tail influences. Correcting these weights reduced the excess back motion while retaining every accepted body and limb parameter.

For rigid ornaments, review connected geometric shells, including duplicated UV/normal seam vertices. Color and position can find candidates, but save explicit reviewed component vertex IDs before mutation. In the Moorland collar, ten shells (277 vertices) were assigned rigidly to `Spine_3`; their geometry, UVs, rest/bind data and shape remain unchanged. Avoid broad neck bounding boxes that also select skin, teeth or ears. Validate each shell's shape and its vertices relative to the attachment bone through the complete exported clip.

Review upper-back ownership separately from collar/neck repair. The pilot removes leg/tail influences only in the reviewed dorsal torso, preserves remaining torso/fur ratios and uses nearby torso donors when no valid influences remain. This is actor-specific, not an automatic repair for all quadrupeds. Preserve a changed-vertex audit and the repair chain back to the immutable original. Source skin repairs do not transfer as category animation.

Measure dorsal motion at fixed calibrated image columns against the reference. A single silhouette score or whole-body damping can conceal the cause. The pilot's anterior-back range changed from 20 to 9 pixels against the reference's 12 pixels; across five columns, range-error average improved from 6.0 to 2.6 pixels. Its body/leg curves and exported limb world transforms were unchanged.

For a less rigid tail, use existing lower/end/tip joints and keep base sway separate. The named wolf chains include `Tail_Mid001`, `Tail_End`, `Tail_Tip`; actor profiles map them to optional `tail_lower`, `tail_end`, `tail_tip` controls. Fit the terminal **fur surface** against reviewed visible endpoints, not a presumed joint. Hind paws can share the lower contour; the pilot's automatic far-right envelope selected the hind foot in some frames. Store reviewed source-only feature IDs, confidence, camera and observation policy.

Looping tail motion may differ from the reference tail's period. Smooth reviewed guides with periodic harmonics at the accepted gait duration and report this regularization. Compare the whole tail shape, local articulation and phase as well as endpoint displacement. The first independent lower/end/tip fit reduced endpoint error from 13.7 to 6.2 pixels, but the user rejected its exaggerated counter-curl. A lower endpoint residual alone is insufficient acceptance evidence.

Constrain the distal section to a coherent bend before fitting its endpoint. Keep inherited parent motion and limit the tip's additional rotation; avoid independent opposite compensation that moves a single point correctly while distorting the silhouette. Prefer several reviewed section landmarks when visible. The corrected pilot uses one smooth bend distributed 55/30/15% through lower/end/tip controls, retaining the original base sway. Source tip-local excursion falls from 37.1 to 1.4 degrees while the endpoint still moves through parent motion. The revised endpoint residual is about 11.6 pixels; it is diagnostic, not a visual acceptance gate. Record the rejected fit and the user's shape preference rather than describing it as exact capture.

Transfer coherent bend intent on each native serial chain, never as copied local quaternions or source skin weights. Map and verify lower/end/tip ancestry. Store a target profile adapter for amplitude, distribution and whether the bend should be centered on its native resting shape. `video_motion.secondary.apply_tail_intent` applies this policy and is used by contact retargeting. The Dire Wolf uses 45% amplitude, 60/28/12% distribution and a native-rest-centered wave; its straighter, more horizontal tail cannot use the source's independent distal curves unchanged. When the accepted gait is unchanged, retain validated native limb parameters and add only secondary motion; otherwise solve contacts again. Verify final exported leg transforms, paw surfaces and tail world transforms independently.

Target proportions can require a static clearance adapter. The rejected independent-tail fit needed a 30-degree Dire Wolf base angle; the coherent revision returns to 25 degrees and still passes clearance. Keep this actor-specific and test the full mesh at keys and between keys; never raise the animal to clear its tail. Inspect loop continuity, skin deformation and return-to-idle reset on final hashed files. Attachment skin repairs can affect other clips' appearance even when their curves are preserved.

Check attachment centering separately from the full rest chain and mesh. The Moorland tail base matches Hips X, but its native rest tail curves sideways: the tip joint is 0.422 native units off that centerline and the fur endpoint is 0.594 units off. The Dire Wolf mapped tail chain is centered throughout. A front isometric camera can hide the Moorland tail behind the torso. Include the opposite isometric, rear quarter and top views, and show unchanged rest geometry with the hip centerline. Do not recenter geometry or infer unobserved lateral motion merely to make one camera clearer.

When the user explicitly requests a centered hanging tail, make a separate actor rest correction rather than changing category motion or flattening mesh width. The Moorland correction uses `video_motion.tail_rest.center_hanging_tail`: straighten the reviewed native chain in the lateral/up plane while preserving segment lengths and rearward offsets, bake its weighted surface and transformed normals, then update rest transforms and inverse binds. Retain every weight, joint index, UV, material, texture and non-tail vertex. Rebase only the existing native tail channels in the other actions, adding default keys where omitted, so action switching returns to the corrected rig. Audit geometry IDs, bind round-trip, centerline and non-tail preservation independently.

Rest straightening can lower the tip even though segment lengths are fixed. The pilot's first candidate brushed the walk floor; a constant one-degree tail-base offset clears it while retaining the accepted sway amplitude/phase and all distal curves. Do not move the body or paws to compensate. The corrected walk has zero non-tail surface difference, and the Dire Wolf transferred surface is unchanged. Sample the full tail at keys and between keys, and compare other-action deformation with its existing baseline: Jog/attack already contain strong local compression. Record those limitations rather than claiming the six actions were artistically polished. The reference's previous rest silhouette is no longer a centering target after an explicit rest-shape change.
