# Contact and stride polish

Use `video_motion/contact.py` and the CLI `polish` command for an existing fitted walking loop. The first implementation requires glTF Y-up, +Z forward, +X depth. This is a reference-guided reconstruction: reviewed visible reach and cadence supply evidence; regularized hidden-limb phases must be labelled as assumptions.

## Diagnose before changing the gait

Measure each paw's forward excursion in the calibrated video and exported model. Review stance runs, touchdown, toe-off and full-stride identities. The wolf pilot's fore-paw runs covered roughly 198–223 pixels and hind reach roughly 268–272 pixels at 227 pixels per model unit. These are pilot-specific observations, not canine defaults. Merged contours, duplicate frames and backward identity assignments can compress an otherwise plausible silhouette fit.

Track the same evaluated sole vertices throughout a stance. Build paw surface sets from foot/toe/claw descendants and nearby low ankle/lower-joint vertices. The Moorland wolf has ground-level heel vertices weighted mainly to its ankle; selecting only foot-owned vertices missed 0.039 model units of penetration. Keep the set and reference point fixed while measuring slide; switching to whichever vertex is lowest can hide sliding.

Add virtual actor advancement to the paw trajectory before judging slip. An in-place planted foot moves backward at the actor's forward speed. A stationary body-relative foot slides in the moving world. Use one shared stance speed for all four feet; target leg-length scaling applied independently to each planted paw produces incompatible support speeds.

## Fit continuous stance and swing

The version-2 contact model defines one speed, per-limb touchdown phase and stance fraction, swing clearance, and optional center offsets. Linear stance moves backward at the declared speed; a smooth Hermite swing returns the paw with matching endpoint velocity. Match visible extrema and swing height before treating smoothness as success. Keep the cycle periodic throughout rather than compressing the last part of the stride to reconcile a bad endpoint track.

Fit actual weighted surface positions and foot orientation with IK while preserving skeleton lengths, rest transforms, mesh and weights. Retain modest body movement; lower the body or adapt reviewed target control bounds when a rest pose makes the desired reach infeasible. Do not silently shorten the shared stride to hide an unreachable contact. Bounds are rig-specific fitting limits, not measured anatomical facts.

Keep paw orientation separate from ankle orientation. The Moorland heel's strong ankle ownership benefits from an ankle-rest orientation constraint. Applying that same constraint to the Dire Wolf overconstrained its rear toe-off, even with wider joint limits. The target profile can reduce `ankle_orientation_weight` while retaining the paw orientation, evaluated-surface contact and sliding gates. Inspect the resulting deformation; changing a constraint is not evidence that the contact passed.

## Intermediate joints and the lifted fold

A correct paw path leaves the elbow pose underdetermined. The first contact polish passed the floor/sliding gates but missed the visible folded foreleg in Meshy. Review the actual upper/lower/foot hinge, its signed bend direction (IK pole), and the lifted paw's orientation. Store reference-specific pole intent and optional `swing_foot_pitch_guide` / `swing_ankle_pitch_guide` in each leg's contact model. Guides use `swing_fractions` from 0 to 1 and `degrees`; their endpoints return to neutral with zero slope. Keep floor locking and flat paw orientation during stance. These are reviewed pose guides, not measured hidden 3D joint angles.

Inspect parents before assigning anatomical meaning to role names. In the Moorland wolf, `Front_Leg_Ankle` and `Front_Leg_Foot` are siblings under `Front_Leg_Lower`. The ankle is a weighted helper rather than the next link of the serial foot chain. Rotating the paw/helper alone to imitate a fold distorted the wrist. Fit the true hinge direction first, then coordinate the helper and terminal paw during swing and inspect the deformed mesh. The Dire Wolf lacks that front helper but still supports the category's fold intent through its native chain.

For a confirmed leaf heel helper sharing the foot's parent, the actor profile can list the forelimb in `paw_follow_helpers`. It then receives the paw's full skin transform: `helper_world = foot_world * inverse(foot_rest_world) * helper_rest_world`. Rotation alone was insufficient and narrowed the paw under a strong fold. Following preserves the paw/heel relationship while the anatomical upper/lower/foot segment lengths remain fixed. The leaf deform-control offset may change; declare it in clip metadata and validate its follow transform at keys and between keys separately from limb lengths. This is a motion correction to a confirmed helper, not permission to stretch anatomical joints or alter bind/rest data or weights.

Pose bounds refer to control rotations relative to a rig's bind pose, not physical elbow flexion. Rest directions differ; enough counter-rotation may be required to keep the paw flat while the forearm bends. Measure the actual exported hinge angle. Choose a consistent, reviewed pole for the cycle; forcing an instantaneous branch switch can require unreachable full extension and produce a pop. The wolf pilot's v8/v9 forward-bowing branch was a mistaken interpretation despite passing the authored pose-guide checks. The corrected v10 uses a backward elbow throughout stance and swing: negative signed hinge around canonical +X depth with +Z forward, and positive backward displacement from the upper-to-foot chord. Validate visible bend direction over the complete cycle; a guide passing its own chosen sign does not establish agreement with the video. The downward terminal paw turn remains an independent airborne constraint.

Report articulation separately from contact: signed true-hinge angles, airborne paw orientation, pose-guide error and mesh shape from all views. The prior wolf export is a useful regression case: it passes contact while failing the new fold-intent check. Preserve stride reach and cadence when changing the intermediate joints. Compare against the reference at its native frame rate and calibrated camera, and retain the previous revision for visual review.

Do not lift the whole animal to the lowest arbitrary mesh point. The Dire Wolf's hanging tail was the rest model's lowest point and caused hovering paws. Solve paw contact first and adapt tail clearance in the target profile. The category gait carries motion intent; rest-pose corrections such as tail angle and body height belong to the actor profile's `contact_adaptation`.

## Shoulder attachment

Paw locking and a correct elbow pole still leave the shoulder offset underdetermined. Compare the deformed foreleg's visible attachment in the calibrated reference frames. Distinguish that skin feature from an anatomical pivot hidden by fur. The wolf pilot uses an authored chest-relative `shoulder_direction_guide`, with a unit `[up, forward]` vector; it is not a measured 3D video landmark. The source upper origins moved roughly 36 pixels rearward and 16–17 pixels downward at the original camera while the established sole paths and cadence were retained.

Solve that direction using each actor's native anchor-to-upper offset rather than copying source shoulder rotations. Inspect feasibility with the full airborne paw tuck: lowering the upper origin changes the counter-rotation needed at the upper, lower and foot controls. A shoulder guide combined with the previous fitting bounds caused heel penetration and pose error. Widen reviewed actor-specific control bounds when appropriate, then recheck actual exported hinge angles, floor contact and deformation. These fitting bounds are not anatomical limits. The Dire Wolf also required body offset -0.38 instead of -0.28 native units to reach the preserved stride with its rearward shoulder placement; this belongs to its actor profile, not the category gait.

Large shoulder rotations can turn shoulder-weighted fur and ornaments even when the limb pose improves. Confirm the weighted ownership. For the wolf pilot, actor profiles use `scapula_offset_controls` on direct anchor/upper pairs whose anchor has no other child. The upper origin follows the same native-length arc; the anchor retains its body-relative orientation, and the upper pose is expressed as animated local translation and rotation. This preserves bind/rest transforms, weights, topology and all anatomical parent distances. It is an actor deformation adapter, not a category-wide assumption or bone-length exemption. Check parent distances between keys, neutral shoulder orientation and action-switch reset channels. A confirmed heel helper still follows the paw separately.

The contact solver can fit a declared `coupled_contact_groups` group simultaneously. Use it when surface constraints or fitting choices interact; do not infer cross-limb weights merely from a contact regression. The pilot's failing heel vertices were weighted to their own leg, and restricted fitting bounds were part of the failure. Retain independent exported per-paw checks even for a simultaneous solve.

An extra joint is optional when the native chain cannot express the needed articulation. In this pilot, the existing foot joint already supplies the downward airborne paw turn, so no new bone was needed. If adding one becomes necessary, declare the changed topology, preserve the rest silhouette/bind deformation, map the new semantic role on transferred actors, and repeat structural, contact, import and runtime checks.

## Local skin defects

Contact and articulation checks cannot detect a misweighted mane. When a visible defect is reported, trace the affected rest vertices and their actual influences before attributing it to a joint or another limb. The Moorland dorsal neck included vertices weighted partly or entirely to hind-leg pelvis controls. The v10 repair removes limb influences in the reviewed neck region, restores nearby torso/fur influences where none remained, and smooths the central mane's abrupt neck/chest transition. Coincident UV/normal seam vertices receive coherent repaired weights without welding geometry.

Use a separate repaired input and `video_motion/repair.py`; the coordinate region and smoothing field are specific to this actor. Retain the immutable source. Record exact changed vertex IDs, original/repaired hashes, normalization, zero unwanted limb mass, unchanged weights outside the region, unchanged geometry/UV/indices/bind/rest data, and unchanged animation curves. The selected motion export then preserves this repaired input. Skin changes can affect other clips' appearance even when their curves are untouched; inspect those separately. The Dire Wolf does not inherit the source's repaired weights.

Independently sample dorsal-neck edge deformation and review the rendered side/front/rear views. The original walk stretched reviewed edges by up to 4.08 times their rest length; the repaired walk's maximum is about 1.10. This is a skin/deformation measurement, separate from floor, sliding and elbow tests. Keep before/after reference comparisons at native cadence and retain rejected candidates.

## Transfer and verify

Version-2 transfer uses the median target/source leg-length ratio as one common step/speed scale. It preserves cadence, touchdown phases and duty factors, then solves the target's own weighted surfaces. Extra target joints remain neutral unless controlled. Store `source_sole_rest_centers`, `source_leg_lengths`, the continuous gait, source controls, provenance and diagnostics in the category package. Keep the target profile alongside it.

Sample exported GLBs at keys and between keys, including stance transitions and loop closure. Report per-paw excursion, stance floor error, moving-world horizontal slip, foot pitch and swing clearance, plus the minimum entire-mesh floor clearance. The pilot's numerical contact gate uses floor error <= 0.2% of actor height, RMS slip <= 1% of speed, 95th-percentile slip <= 2% of speed and foot pitch <= 3 degrees; these are declared pilot tolerances, not universal production thresholds.

Review normal-speed side, front, rear and game views. A moving-ground preview travels backward at each actor's declared speed and makes support locking easier to see. Preview GIF frame delays must sum to the authored duration. Run fresh Blender reimport and an isolated Godot playback/action-switch check on the final hashed exports. Preserve the earlier delivery for comparison and keep runtime replacement as a separate accepted step.
