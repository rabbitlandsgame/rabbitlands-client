## Physical rig and surface mapping

Use explicit node IDs, rest positions and skin ownership. The source's named right tail tip actually carries a hind-leg root, while some named upper/ankle controls are shifted along the physical chain. Crystal Polar Bear has foot/claw helpers with misleading torso/face names. Owlbear has an extra rear distal segment. Map shoulder/elbow/wrist and hip/stifle/hock first; keep extra distal segments and claw descendants with their native paw section unless a separate articulation is intended.

An imported hierarchy need not be an anatomical hierarchy. Obsidian Bear parents foreleg attachments under a facial bone. Applying facial FK to those shoulders gives the wrong motion even with correct names and lengths. Construct the intended anatomical body and shoulder positions in world space, evaluate native physical chains, then encode those world transforms through the unchanged imported hierarchy. Check physical segment lengths separately from nonphysical parent connectors. Do not turn a virtual attachment solution into an unreported rest-rig edit.

Keep one common anatomical scale and stance speed for all four limbs. Transfer source world motion and paw trajectories, rather than local quaternions. Plant the target's actual skinned sole and heel, with a native stance orientation; source contact-correction angles can rock a target with different claw shape and mixed weights. Blend the airborne orientation guide smoothly out of and back into that planted orientation. A floor minimum alone does not lock the sole centroid or certify negligible sliding.

Trace low physical paw vertices that are missing from an influence-based mask. In this pilot, Obsidian Bear had low hind-paw vertices dominated by torso and unrelated limb weights. The separate prepared input repairs 80 vertices, retains original position arrays/rest/binds and all original animation curves, and records the submicrometer neutral-skin numerical difference. Compare the final animation against that prepared input and separately disclose the repair relative to runtime. Do not silently copy those weights to other actors.

Do not constrain a lifted, rotating paw to its standing sole-centroid height. That conflicts with the minimum clearance of a differently shaped paw and can make a whole-body floor projection raise the actor, lose contact and jump back at touchdown. Lock sole height in true stance; during swing, fit clearance and native orientation with the centroid free to rise as its geometry requires. Reproduce the exact failing frames before a dense rebuild.

A nearly straight source limb does not supply a reliable animated cross-product normal. Its sign can change while a proportionally different target still needs a visibly bent knee, creating a large one-frame fold despite accurate sole positions at adjacent keys. Transport a signed, reviewed anatomical hinge plane with the body for the supported solve, then keep released articulation continuous. Check world joint paths and the interpolated skin in addition to key-only paw residuals.

## Fall, release and settled support

Reuse the actual final constrained native support pose at release, including corrections applied by full-skin clearance projection. Caching an earlier solution before that final projection still produces a release snap. Blend the source-relative native limb correction continuously into released FK; do not restart a new IK branch or a raw rest-seeded fold after a paw lifts.

Fit the native settled body, head and limb tuck. A proportionally scaled source corpse can remain propped on a thigh or leave the head floating. Crystal Polar Bear's ice plates are genuine support geometry, so a body ownership mask that excludes the upper thigh plates misclassifies a grounded corpse. Include the physically relevant body/armor support surface while checking the complete skin for penetration.

Separate neck lowering from skull aim when needed. Obsidian Bear needs its native neck and skull controls to bring the head down without turning the muzzle back under the chest. Contact constraints alone can drive one neck control to an excessive angle. Review the actual muzzle direction, visible neck deformation and normal-speed fall from both sides, front/rear, top and game cameras.

A target can need its own rear stance center and standing height to retain the common full excursion. Crystal Polar Bear's hock rests farther behind its hip; copying its native paw center left the back of Jog unreachable. Adapt that center/height explicitly, rather than shortening stride or raising the torso to compensate for a clipped paw.

For a thick forearm, grounded paw coordinates can coexist with an elbow plate through the floor. A bounded outward elbow swivel can clear the native surface while retaining the paw and physical segment lengths. Keep a posture prior so this extra degree of freedom does not drift at the loop seam. After support release, project clearance on the released limb itself, retaining its evaluated native bend plane and rotation guides. Confirm the projection reproduces its seed with zero correction: an unsigned cross-product normal can select the opposite elbow branch.

Exclude separately constrained planted paw surfaces from the body-floor projection. Trying to lift the whole actor to repair a planted-paw residual can create a feedback loop, overextend the support legs and produce a discontinuity at release. Recheck the complete exported skin afterward; that exclusion is not permission for paw penetration.

## Contact boundaries and temporal corrections

Keep exact touchdown and lift-off timestamps in the export grid, including events that wrap the gait phase through zero. Float32 time conversion can put a mathematically exact touchdown just below zero; use a consistent small phase tolerance and canonicalize that phase before evaluating its sole goal. Dense keys alone do not fix a discontinuous contact-to-swing correction. Extend the actual native local stance correction smoothly into the adjacent swing and check half-keys on both sides.

When a whole-skin projection raises the torso while paws support the body, remember the actual body lift at release as well as the limb rotations. Dropping that lift before clearing the released leg makes the free-limb solver correct the same ground collision again, yielding a double correction even if the torso is subsequently raised. Carry the native body lift continuously and let it decay at a bounded rate.

If a native clearance optimizer chatters later in a fall, preserve the selected successful clips rather than rebuilding them all. A short, explicitly recorded native temporal smoothing pass is a candidate correction, not a completed validation: it can introduce floor penetration and destroy planted contacts. Reproject the actual skinned soles, preserve physical local lengths, fit a continuous anticipatory skin-floor envelope, and rerun key/half-key, terminal support and multi-angle checks. Do not qualify the preprojection smoothed checkpoint.

Choose the native death stance against the whole supported interval, not just the starting pose. A target with a higher hip path can need a fixed hind sole closer under its body to retain the source support duration without reaching a straight-leg singularity. Solve that constant native stance explicitly, retain a bend margin, record the displacement, and review the starting silhouette. This is separate from gait stride amplitude. A fixed native foot plane can be more stable than adding a hard ankle/minimum-height equation: the latter may drive a mixed-weight paw into a different IK solution. Compare the full evaluated skin and contact path before selecting the additional degree of freedom.
