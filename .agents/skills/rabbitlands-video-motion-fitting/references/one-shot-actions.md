# Attack and death pilots

Start from the user-accepted animated source/target GLBs, not the uncorrected originals or a repaired rig that lacks the accepted Walk. Copy them as immutable inputs in a new action stage. The wolf stage is `.local/asset-authoring/workspace/generate_models/wolf_action_motion_pilot/`; its source copies retain the accepted centered tail, neck/collar/back skin repairs, and all seven actions. Render the corrected model again and calibrate that image, keeping its geometry/camera hashes with each reference video.

Use `Sword_Attack` for the creature's primary attack slot and `Death_A` for death; the slot name does not imply a weapon. The first wolf attack is one forward lunge and bite, with anticipation, contact and recovery. The first death is one support-loss collapse toward the camera onto its side, followed by a motionless hold. Start with one five-second Meshy video per action, fixed full-body side view, neutral background, no camera change or repeated action. Event times remain unset until the video has been reviewed. Record exact image hashes, prompts, settings, displayed credits, authorization and task IDs before downloading and fitting.

Inspect actual weighted subtrees rather than looking only for a bone named Jaw or relying on joint heights. The Moorland has a native `Chin` child of `Nose`; its lower-jaw origin uses the reviewed native-length Nose-to-Chin arc. The Dire Wolf's `Bone_049`/`Bone_048` owns the **upper snout**, while `Bone_051`/`Bone_050` owns the **lower jaw**. An ownership-colored rest view corrected the first pilot's mistaken classification. On the Dire Wolf, map jaw to `Bone_051`, keep its native pivot fixed (`action_jaw_arc=false`), remove jaw followers, and leave the upper snout with `Head`. Do not transfer the source's hinge translation policy solely because both actors have a jaw. Test lower-jaw opening, stationary upper snout relative to the head, reconstructed local transforms and parent distances. If a rig lacks usable ownership, make any required topology/weight correction a separate reviewed input. Head/neck reach and a bite event alone do not establish matching jaw articulation.

Do not choose a recurrent gait cycle for a one-shot. Review the complete anticipation-to-recovery or collapse-to-settle interval, including the final pose. Keep original cadence in comparisons and event times in seconds and frames. A standing recovery endpoint and a held fallen endpoint are different contracts. Do not reconcile death back to frame zero, apply periodic smoothing, or use periodic spline boundaries. Declare trimming or any later retiming and preserve the contact fraction.

The looping walk fitter is planar and its version-2 contact model assumes recurring paw phases plus a single stance speed. Attack needs explicit support windows and planted-paw positions rather than a treadmill. Sideways death needs body roll, a downward trajectory and support changing from paws to chest/hips/head. `video_motion.actions.ActionRig` supplies native hip-pivot body roll, optional lateral limb tuck, head counter-roll, tail settling and existing-joint jaw arcs. `bake_action` retains one-shot endpoints with nonperiodic interpolation and no unconditional floor lift. Single side video does not identify lateral movement uniquely; record toward-camera roll/tuck/counter-roll as authored choices. The legacy CLI's `--one-shot` flag alone does not supply these controls or contact semantics.

Check limb bend signs in the rotating body/limb frame. A correct native elbow appears to change its world-camera sign when the body passes ninety degrees during a fall. Keep the native rear knee/hock branches stable during landing; sparse angular interpolation between two IK branches can drive a knee through the floor even when all solved keys clear it. Polish actual weighted support surfaces densely and independently inspect between-key poses. Include upper-limb vertices in floor checks, not just the lowest rest-paw subset.

Fit the actual weighted support surfaces. Attack contact checks separate braced hind paws, airborne forepaws, landing and recovery. For death, track paw support loss, body-floor contact and the settled pose; keep chest, hip, head and tail above the floor and prevent settled-surface drift. Do not use a per-frame whole-actor lift to conceal a support failure. Independent checks must cover keys, midpoints, deformation, velocity at impact, hold stillness and the end pose.

Package semantic motion intent, support windows, event times, body orientation and endpoint policy separately from actor adapters. Retarget on native geometry, segment lengths and joint axes; source skin/rest corrections stay with the source. Retain the accepted Walk and every unrelated action exactly relative to the declared action input. Check source and Dire Wolf at the same speed from side, game/opposite game, rear and ground-level views. Run fresh Blender reimport and Godot playback, including attack-to-Idle and death-to-Idle/reset behavior on exact final hashes. Numerical validity and user artistic acceptance remain separate.

## Contact lessons from the wolf action pilots

Determine the standing baseline from the native evaluated soles. The Dire Wolf's rest tail reaches the model's lowest point while its paws are about 0.162 native units higher; the whole-mesh minimum therefore gives the wrong paw baseline. Combine the sole-height adapter with native tail clearance, then fit contacts. The accepted Dire Wolf walk's 25-degree tail clearance was a useful actor-specific starting point, not a universal canine setting.

Support regions must correspond to the actual surface named in the report. A loose head-ownership threshold included neck fur shared with the chest and falsely certified a floating muzzle. The corrected wolf head patch uses more than 65% head-subtree ownership and lies forward of the native head pivot; it does not overlap the chest support patch. Distal tail ownership defines a separate fallen-tail support. Inspect each patch's actual vertices and bounds on a new actor; thresholds alone do not establish anatomical correspondence.

Solve the settled torso, muzzle, tail and limb clearance together. Longer target limbs can prop up the torso even after a torso-only solve converges. Fold the native limbs as part of the fallen pose. Preserve coherent tail sections before that solve; later clamps, section redistribution or independent limb corrections can break previously solved supports. Body height can be a variable in a physical contact solve during an unsupported fall; an after-the-fact whole-actor lift that moves planted paws remains inappropriate.

Contact at solved poses does not establish contact between them. Fast recovery produced perfectly planted key poses but visible drift between sparse angular samples. Refine affected stance/landing intervals, then sample the exported skin at keys and midpoints. Keep reference cadence (24 fps here), fitting cadence and export key density separate. Dense pilot exports may need a separately validated reduction before runtime integration. Do not relax contact tolerances to make a failing transfer pass.

Unwrap equivalent foot-angle revolutions before nonperiodic interpolation. A roughly 290-degree representation jump was an interpolation artifact rather than a real motion of the paw. Preserve anatomical bend branches in the rotating limb frame, and check airborne heel/ankle-weighted vertices as well as sole positions. The target's brief rear-paw reposition needed its own flight-surface solve; transferring angular intent alone let the heel cross the floor.

For iterative fitting, `Rig.pose(..., world_only=True)` and `ActionRig.pose(..., world_only=True)` avoid reconstructing unused local quaternions. **Only the third return value (world transforms) is valid in that mode**; use the full default path for local T/Q export. Tests compare the fast world transforms with the full exported-transform reconstruction on both native rigs, including roll, splay, jaw arcs, shoulder offsets and heel helpers. `SurfacePatches` caches exact native skin subsets for anatomy/clearance solves; independently compare their points with the complete evaluated skin.

Review the early collapse separately from the final grounded hold. The first wolf pilot passed contact and terminal checks while remaining too upright at the reference's foreleg buckle. Check chest/head descent and signed foreleg flexion at the **same original timestamps**. The corrected early phase uses native standing-height fractions and explicit backward-elbow preferences, followed by sole support IK on each actor. These heights and angles are authored guides, not measurements of hidden joints. Fit torso/head skin clearance before limbs, check the complete forearm/knee fur as well as soles, and solve unsupported body/limb clearance together during release. A lowered joint center alone can put collar/neck fur below the floor. Retain the existing roll/settled pose outside the correction interval, and verify exact terminal-pose preservation. Coarse visual guides can initialize dense contact refinement; reference fps stays independent of solve/export density.

Separate authored elbow folding from final sole locking. In the wolf's supported head-drop phase, retaining the elbow control and solving native anchor/upper/foot for sole height, forward position and pitch avoided a weighted compromise that left paw drift. Use continuous seeds and check anatomical bend branches. Increasing export density alone did not fix undersampled semantic contact parameters: refine the native contact poses at the required density, then independently measure exported skin at keys and half-key times. Choose density from the failure intervals rather than applying the pilot's 1536 Hz solve rate to every animation. `.local/asset-authoring/workspace/generate_models/wolf_action_motion_pilot/finish_revision2.py` records this wolf correction; `verify_contact_midpoints_v2.py` checks the resulting surfaces at 3072 Hz. Comparisons remain at the original 24 fps.

The reference comparisons retain the original camera scale and 24 fps timestamps. Horizontal camera padding can reveal native motion extending outside the original frame; apply corresponding background padding to the reference and record it. A 121-frame movie displays the endpoint at 5.0 seconds for one additional frame, giving 5.0417 seconds of playback. That endpoint display is not a retimed action.

Check imported engine curves as well as exported GLBs. The initial Godot fidelity probe showed tiny terminal attack drift after the default animation optimization, despite exact held GLB endpoints. Disable optimizer/compression on the isolated probe's actual AnimationPlayer import ID, then reimport and retest; preserve the original result as evidence. Any later runtime key reduction or import optimization needs its own contact/hold validation. See [Godot's advanced import settings](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/advanced_import_settings.html) for the importer controls; verify names against the installed version.
