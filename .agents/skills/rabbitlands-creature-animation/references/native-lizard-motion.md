## Whole palms and native scale

Fit the contact frame from spatially separated physical underside regions spanning toes and heel. For these rigs, four support regions are sampling patches, not a claim that the mesh has four toes. Carry the fitted neutral frame through swing, release, landing and supported collapse. Qualify all four palms, including the rear heels, with normal tilt, underside height spread, complete-palm velocity and whole-surface clearance. Keep native pad thickness and claw shape.

Check reach using actual chain lengths. Crests and a tall frill can enlarge total bounds without adding limb reach; a shared normalized root drop must still pass each actor's reach and floor checks. Retain each actor's stature, native lanes and rest-tail profile. Bind measured stride speed to the actual registry scale; normalized review display height is separate from game scale.

## Eyes, frill, crest and throat flap

Inspect welded topology and physical attachment before changing weights. Detached eye islands should remain coherent with the face. A front-to-back spatial head mask alone can capture forward forepaw toes; gate by native height and anatomy, then verify all-surface clearance.

Give the visible appendage a coherent owner and feather its root into the surrounding neck/torso. A binary spatial selection can create an abrupt head-to-shoulder boundary even when rigid face and torso-core tests pass. The Iguana's throat flap exposed this in local edge-strain diagnostics; a continuous lateral, longitudinal and height transition removed the severe discontinuity. The Frilled Lizard's lower fan needs ownership below its outer fan, where an upper-only mask can leave cloth-like tissue following the foreleg. The Chameleon's crest needs the same continuous base transition.

Keep coincident rows welded across UV seams, retain all influence sets, and distinguish rigid appendage cores from their intentionally blended roots. Audit local edge stretch/shrink and explicit transition probes alongside face rigidity, torso volume and seam checks. Inspect the strained patch at matching times and from several angles; a global pass or an isolated edge maximum cannot replace that inspection.
