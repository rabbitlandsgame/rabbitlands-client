# Basilisk and serpent motion pilots

Use for the current imported Viridian Basilisk and Cobra, or another explicitly selected actor with verified comparable anatomy. These pilots remain drafts until human review and deliberate runtime promotion. They share an audit framework, not the same locomotion constraints.

## Short splayed quadrupeds

The basilisk's tall head crest and raised spiked tail enlarge its bounds without adding leg reach. Measure the visible upper/lower segments independently for all four limbs. Check both the maximum reach and the minimum radius `abs(upper - lower)` throughout stance, swing and collapse. A whole paw lifted close to a short thigh can cross the minimum radius even when the leg never stretches.

Center paired contact lanes relative to the torso and hip anchors, then measure actual exported left/right touchdown advances under one common travel speed. Native asymmetry can put a hind pair too far behind the hip; shortening every step is not the only remedy. Fit a modest forward lane adjustment and sufficient lateral clearance, keep the two sides mirrored, and use a lower hind-paw recovery when the rear chains are short. Avoid a generic fore/hind lift height. During death, release the complete feet along a smooth outward path that stays above the minimum fold radius; keep native segment lengths and floor clearance. Verify the resulting stance visually rather than accepting reach alone.

For the turned skull, align the actual snout/head anatomy, not crest PCA. Move the eyes, tongue and crest coherently, feathering the neck attachment. Preserve the tail's raised vertical character while centering it horizontally. Transport cross sections and normals into a versioned prepared input, preserving tail guide lengths, original node/rest/bind prefixes, textures and static skin. Use anatomical ownership for the spiked tail: a longitudinal spatial mask can capture the nearby hind legs. Keep raw-to-prepared changes separate from prepared-to-export preservation.

## Legless serpents

Do not apply paw, sole or alternating footfall gates to a snake. Inventory the native body, tail and raised neck chains. A traveling lateral body wave should preserve vertebral segment lengths and taper toward the tail; keep the raised hood directed forward with restrained lateral tracking. Faster slither can change wave amplitude and cadence without bouncing the head.

Inspect native underside sections before selecting support probes. The Cobra's posterior taper is naturally raised while its central belly is grounded. Audit that raised region for clearance, and use physically grounded belly patches to measure continuous support. Record the distinction; do not claim that the entire underside is a stationary planted foot. The review grid represents proposed travel speed, not a paw-slip certificate.

Measure the visible neck rather than assuming its native bone centers are anatomical. The Cobra rig missed the visible S bend by a substantial fraction of the body thickness. A source-preserving prepared input appends a measured body/neck control chain; the original geometry, skin and node/rest/bind prefixes remain exact. Fit low body cross sections and narrow axial neck envelopes separately, excluding the low torso from the neck-height samples.

Transport cross sections through continuous curve frames to retain neck thickness. Keep the actual skull, eyes and tongue coherent while allowing the broad hood to bend. Interpolate rotations rather than linearly mixing disparate point transforms. When ground support is needed, lift whole cross-section neighborhoods; clamping individual vertices can flatten the neck into a thin strip. Preserve the original static skin arrays and report the prepared rig separately from raw-source preservation. New controls also need explicit attachment mapping at future runtime promotion. During death, fold the neck forward and lower the hood nearly flat onto the ground. Measure full-surface clearance, hood support, hood-plane angle, local stretch and shrink, seam continuity, surface velocity and the held ending. A grounded tail does not certify a supported hood.

A neck solver can preserve every segment length while still producing a sharp or unstable bend. Constraining the terminal tangent consumed too much reach in the Cobra strike trial and worsened local shrink and velocity. Inspect the whole path, not just its endpoint. A whole-body forward lunge with modest neck projection was smoother. Native-chain endpoint fitting still produced a visible pinch despite passing broad strain gates; the final pilot uses the measured surface chain and transported sections. Keep rejected trials and their measurements, and avoid relaxing strain or continuity limits to accept them.

## Export and review

Reconstruct rigid hood/paw cores exactly while regularizing soft transition rows. Bind temporal surface caches to the selected source and processor hashes. Sample fast, tightly curved one-shots densely enough to reproduce their guides between keys; the Cobra needed a higher export cadence than the previous grounded quadruped default. Keep the replay tolerance unchanged and rerun the final exported skin, normals and current-importer checks. A passing authored guide is not proof that the exported file passes.

Provide native-speed, six-angle reviews, full pose sheets, and appropriate support close-ups for each anatomy. Keep concepts, numerical evidence, visual pose inspection, encoded-video checks, offline control tests, live browser playback and human acceptance as separate states. Large authoring exports require runtime optimization before integration. Save a shared review without altering earlier pending reviews or treating a previous upload approval as permission to publish new creatures.
