## Goose and Swan transfers

Treat low webbed-foot components as anatomical units. The Swan source has overlapping named leg influence and asymmetrically placed feet; sign-of-X and strongest-source-weight selection both split its medial webbing. Identify separate connected low-leg surfaces through welded triangle topology, then calibrate each visible shin and align its target path. Give the complete bare shin physical ownership before blending into that side's thigh domain. Pin populated middle-shin probes and whole feet while repairing the surrounding transition. A lateral-coordinate cutoff can leave the inner thigh immobile even after the outer silhouette looks fixed.

The Swan's rear S-neck extends behind its forward neck field. Include that complete branch: excluding posterior neck rows leaves torso-owned shelves below the skull during a jab or collapse. Inspect side and opposite-side images in addition to edge strain; a bounded edge ratio alone does not certify a smooth silhouette. Keep head rotation consistent with the adjacent neck section while maintaining the supported breast.

Keep a released foot on a continuous reach branch. When the target reaches a two-segment chain's minimum radius, forcing its lateral coordinate to a particular sign can jump the hock across the body. A radial correction along the current displacement preserves continuity. Smooth hock-floor constraints before they activate; inspect the complete fall densely, including its maximum point speed and held endpoint.

Do not infer head facing from an unweighted bill-vertex average relative to a generic head guide. Unequal tessellation and the offset guide can imply a turn even when the visible head keeps its source direction. Record the source geometric axis, its absolute posed yaw and its change from that reference separately. A rigid fit to fixed bill surface probes measures added rotation without vertex-density bias; use front and side views to decide whether the source itself needs straightening.

Record exact authored-key replay separately from between-key interpolation. An affine basis can reproduce fitted source keys closely while locally constrained skin differs more between exported keys. Bind both measurements to the selected model and keep fresh engine/Blender fidelity as independent checks. This does not replace strain, contact, anatomical support or visual review.
