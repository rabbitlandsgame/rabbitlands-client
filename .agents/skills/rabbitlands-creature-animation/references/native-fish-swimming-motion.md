# Native fish swimming pilots

Use the Abyssal Armored Fish workflow for a deliberately selected fish with comparable anatomy, after inspecting its own source. It shares numerical and review tools with swimming mammals and pectoral crawlers. It does not share their propulsion: this fish drives a lateral caudal stroke, rather than a dolphin's vertical fluke stroke or a Mudskipper's ground contacts. Seahorses and rays require their own assessment.

## Motion and supported ending

Use steady heavy-trunk cruise, a stronger/faster posterior wave, restrained pectoral steering, a short armored-head/body ram and a forward settlement. Keep native three-dimensional guide lengths while changing guide directions and heights. Separate idle centering from the intentional lateral swim stroke. Swimming preview travel speed is authored, not a measured planted-foot stride.

Fold or relax the ventral blade before the body reaches the bottom. A clear fin tip or grounded chin does not certify the torso. Audit disjoint actual pelvic, belly, chest and cranial underside regions, excluding the blade and steering-fin tips. Lateral sides of a rounded belly are naturally higher than its central underside; do not demand that an entire side wall be a contact patch. A bounded fit of pelvic and cranial guide heights can settle those regions without scaling segments or clamping source vertices. ## Qualification

Rigid facial and distal-fin checks, serial guide lengths, cross-section/volume appearance, whole-surface strain, local/world angular rates, contact regions and the held ending are separate gates. Strong static centering can stretch a skin edge even during Idle. Inspect worst exported edge IDs and source ownership: the armored fish needed broader caudal and keel-root transitions, with its rigid cores retained, rather than a wider strain tolerance.

Qualify the selected exported keys and between-key skin, exact raw preparation and full authored-motion replay, then independent fresh Blender/current-Godot bone, surface and normal witnesses. Inspect all six angles of each action, with particular attention to fin roots, armor volume, keel release, chin/body support and neutral tail alignment. Keep drafts, visual inspection, browser playback, human approval and runtime installation as distinct recorded states.
