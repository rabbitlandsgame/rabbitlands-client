## Salamander transfer lessons

Cave Salamander has three actual toe pads per paw, despite extra terminal controls below one right forefoot digit. Select physical digit underside patches from visible geometry and ownership; the number of leaf bones is not the number of toes. Retain its longer native serial chains and outward bend planes while fitting the accepted action timing.

Inspect the full heel behind the wrist or ankle. A low Salamander heel retained roughly 40 percent lower-leg weight and penetrated the floor while rigid toe targets passed. Make the complete low palm/heel coherent with its foot, then feather into the carpal/hock transition and preserve welded rows. Recheck the whole surface and all toe pads after this preparation.

Distinguish exactly rigid contact tissue from softly blended wrist skin above it. Near-unit ownership alone can include an upper wrist vertex and falsely classify normal flex as paw sliding. Exclude a blend transition from the rigid-contact metric only after confirming it is outside the physical low palm/heel and remains clearly above the floor throughout support. Retain those vertices as independent surface probes and report their clearance. A blended low heel requires a skin repair, not a narrower audit mask. Apply rigid face/body constraints to exact rigid cores; qualify intentional neck/gill transitions with skin witnesses and local deformation checks.
