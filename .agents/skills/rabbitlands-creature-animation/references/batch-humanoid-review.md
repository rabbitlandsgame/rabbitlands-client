# Batch humanoid draft reviews

Use this reference when preparing several unapproved humanoid families together. The eight-family batch described in private continuation memory is a draft collection, not an approved or installed animation manifest.

## Preserve useful current work

Compare the actual current models before applying a whole-body donor. Several imported armed actors already have independent anatomical rigs, equipment grips, capes and usable weapon-specific attacks. Retain passing actions and make targeted revisions where the evidence identifies a defect. A broad retarget can introduce abdomen, shoulder or cloth defects into an otherwise useful source. Original node names and hierarchy are clues, not proof that a native pivot lies at the visible anatomical joint.

Read the registry's actual animation map and enumerate every native clip. Five golems carry a dedicated ground-pound attack beside a legacy attack slot; several winged actors also retain flight. Select the intended primary attack for the visible review, and include all retained clips in independent import witnesses. Do not assume that every actor has exactly seven animations or silently rename a dedicated action to fit a fixed test.

## Physical probes and local repairs

Keep physical probe sets fixed across revisions. Hand and leg triangle sets must be disjoint; inherited cross-limb weights can otherwise classify the same leg surface as both a hand and a leg. Check anatomical placement and connected surface membership when expanding probes for a sculpted hand with no dedicated hand weights. A numerical clearance result does not replace the front, side and opposite views.

A small shoulder-neighborhood mask can leave a hard boundary where mixed forearm/torso influences continue farther along the arm. Weld UV-equivalent points and inspect the actual worst stretched edges before choosing the repair region. Continuous topology-based arm transitions helped several draft actors, but should not become a blanket repaint of every actor's torso or limbs. Preserve unaffected rows and recheck all actions after a skin change.

Existing corrective shapes depend on their original skin weights. In particular, changing a morph-corrected actor's ownership can magnify distortion even when the new weights appear smoother. One draft alternative is a bounded, action-specific surface correction: evaluate the posed skin, regularize the identified stretched edges, and map the small world-space correction back through the actual blended skin transform. Record its affected vertices and maximum displacement. Preserve original geometry, skin and unrelated targets, explicitly clear added targets in unrelated actions, and test both shape keys and between-key poses. This is a candidate authoring technique, not a reason to accept an unreviewed deformation or impose a universal displacement limit.

## Released limb rotation

A smooth wrist relative to its forearm can still wind in world space when the forearm itself rolls. Measure both relative and world rotation paths through death. For a released arm, parallel transport of the forearm frame along the unchanged elbow/wrist route can remove unnecessary axial winding. Distribute the shortest residual terminal roll smoothly so the original ending orientation remains exact. Check wrist-position preservation, the complete hand surface, equipment and any blend controls after the change.

## Import and review evidence

An independent importer can differ between coarse quaternion keys despite agreeing at the keys. Exact SLERP subdivision that retains the original knot union can reduce those differences without changing the native motion. Deduplicate timestamps after float32 conversion, then prove off-grid bone and skin preservation before replacing the candidate. Write a temporary output first so failed validation cannot overwrite the selected file.

Inspect actual nonzero influence counts. Some current authoring rigs store twelve or sixteen slots and have more than eight nonzero influences, while the client mesh path exposes eight. Agreement between two client imports cannot establish preservation if both lose the same influences. Keep this as an explicit promotion issue; do not silently discard weights or describe a complete authoring preview as game-ready.

Bind each report and media package to the final file hash. Blender can exit successfully after a script assertion; require the completion marker, receipt and expected frame counts. Use fresh output report names or verify hashes to reject stale engine results. Static weapons need their native attachment frame during rest mapping, rather than whichever animated frame the attachment currently displays. Distinguish sampled motion checks, complete six-angle media, strict client compatibility and human acceptance. A review-only batch remains local and pending until the user authorizes a separate publication or integration step.
## Grounding and complete-arm audits

A lowest-point floor test can accept a corpse balanced on its head while its torso floats. Probe the visible chest and pelvis separately from the head, horns, hands, clothing and tail, and inspect the final support from the side and rear. Fit articulated release and whole-body settling after planted support ends; retain native segment lengths. A compound hock/pastern needs its own released hoof orientation. Making the whole hoof inherit the shin can push a leg through the floor and lift the body again when floor correction runs. Rooted or armored bodies can legitimately rest on their supporting branches or plates; record the actual support geometry rather than treating every pelvis gap identically.

Hand-only clearance can miss an elbow or forearm passing through a thigh. Extend travel probes to complete anatomical arms, using fixed, disjoint arm and leg regions and checking source cross-limb weight leakage. Classify the physical model first: a ghost robe, root skirt, spirit tail or asymmetric stone base is not a pair of walking legs just because its controls have thigh/calf names. Keep those anatomy exceptions explicit and inspect the accessory motion separately. Small shoulder adjustments helped several physical biped drafts; compare their silhouette as well as intersection counts before choosing a large sideways flare.

Measure tail centering on the visible surface in the pelvis/body frame. Centered guide bones can still leave a biased tail surface. A small calibrated attachment offset can retain internal tail lengths and rotations while centering the actual field. Check the base attachment and several longitudinal regions, retain normal sway, and distinguish true tails from loincloth or belt chains with tail names.

## Corrective-shape portability and media reuse

A signed compressed corrective basis can match the native reader while an importer clamps its weights. In this batch, positive time-local pose shapes imported faithfully in Blender after the signed alternative failed. Keep the surface correction bounded, pin complete support surfaces where appropriate, retain original targets, and preserve the old morph time-knot union when composing channels. Test the actual target engine rather than assuming native or Blender agreement proves portability. A dedicated attack can still have a client morph-track gap even when all ordinary clips pass; keep that separate from local preview qualification and fix it before promotion.

Reusing an old action video after another clip changes is valid only with proof that its geometry, skin, relevant morphs and action curves are identical. Record the original media/model binding and the preservation proof; render and decode every changed review action. Refresh numerical, fresh-import and client receipts against the final combined model hash. Do not turn a complete local review into an implicit approval, upload or runtime replacement.

## Creature personality passes

Write per-creature movement, attack and death concepts before a broad style pass, and expose the text beside the review. Distinguish new pose construction from timing changes. Cadence, anticipation, impact and recovery can differentiate existing qualified trajectories without discarding repaired anatomy. A concept describes this draft's visible behavior; do not imply new flight, disintegration or physics effects that were not implemented.

When changing timing alone, drive all joint, scale and shape channels from one monotone source clock. Insert every piecewise-linear clock corner into each linear sampler, retain original knots and normalize unique timestamps after float32 conversion. This preserves the same physical pose path through time. Mapping each channel's sparse key times independently through a curved clock can desynchronize feet, torso and corrective shapes. Cubic spline channels need a specific tangent/resampling adapter; do not treat them as ordinary linear curves. Update gait duration, measured ground speed, action holds and review timing together. Bind any retimed existing renders to a numerical source-pose equivalence proof and disclose their source-frame quantization; changed poses require fresh renders.

Forward gaze must be calibrated from physical facial features or head symmetry, not solely a native head bone. Distribute a correction through the neck/head without changing rest binds, and verify the visible face relative to the body frame. A standing correction can push a snout through the floor during collapse. Release it naturally before head contact or refit supported death; lifting the whole corpse can recreate floating torso support.

For a skeleton breakup, audit welded connected mesh components first. Separate existing intact bone islands rather than stretching a continuous skin between detached joints. Rigid island trajectories can be baked as positive time-local shape targets for a preview; keep original targets and unrelated drivers, pin grounded supports and inspect between-key rigidity. Fit pile placement, floor contact, bounds and terminal stillness. This preview method does not establish runtime morph support or rigid-body physics and needs independent client qualification before promotion.

A horn attack also needs mane/shoulder clearance during neck recovery. Broad ownership probes can share blended attachment faces; retain their diagnostic counts and separately inspect disjoint physical regions. Clamp excessive neck recoil and prefer the native arm bend as a counterweight when an unrelated IK frame introduces seam contact. Recheck horns, muzzle, mane and arms from several angles as well as the complete planted hooves.


## Spatial personality and support references

A request for creature-specific poses and paths needs geometric edits, not only a source-clock change. Write individual posture, shoulder/forearm arcs, airborne leg bends and fall directions, then measure actual world-joint and surface differences for each review action. Preserve useful native equipment grips, bone lengths and unrelated clips. Keep airborne foot changes gated away from stance; verify complete physical support fields rather than ankle positions alone. Render every changed action from the selected final model.

Bake whole-body edits into descendants of actual skeleton roots. Rotating both a skeleton and its unexported wrapper can cancel the intended change when local channels are reconstructed. Preserve static attachment worlds and test separate weapon meshes through an independent importer.

An old floor defect can survive a timing-only preservation proof. Screen the whole fall, not just the terminal pose, and correct released feet through their native chains while keeping the torso supported. A smooth world hand can still be paired with excessive forearm roll. Parallel transport the forearm along its unchanged elbow/wrist route, compensate child local channels to retain world hand/equipment poses and distribute the shortest terminal roll. Recheck the complete skin and floor afterward.

Physical root appendages may have coarse leg controls that cannot serve as ordinary ankle targets. A foot IK trial can introduce a sharp shin swing even when floor clearance passes. Keep a stable native control path and fit a versioned physical support field where that better describes the visible root anatomy. Disjoint paw fields must retain their full union; shared vertices cannot be driven as both feet. Bone-distance ownership alone is unreliable for off-axis native pivots. Record preparation changes and retain the original input.

Do not close a gait by resetting only its final key. Prepare a continuous native-cycle correction, including corrective-shape drivers, and qualify contact against that explicit prepared source. A discontinuous old endpoint is not a valid preserved-contact reference for a newly closed loop. Keep any added shape weights compatible with the importer; positive convex blends avoid signed-weight clamping.

A low hanging sculpted hand can be near the knee and contain leg weight leakage. Nearest-bone or broad height-based calf repainting can pull the hand into the leg. Inspect the actual mesh from multiple angles and use coherent pose-space surface transport with topology, support and attachment constraints when necessary. Bounded edge strain does not itself establish clearance. A small native shoulder adjustment can finish clearance while preserving corrected muscle volume. Keep rejected local repairs separate from the selected fit and bind all evidence to the final hash.


## Visible thighs and forward collapse paths

A moving thigh control does not prove the visible thigh moves. Audit physical surface ownership: the Skeleton’s femur islands were mostly assigned to the pelvis while its virtual knees already moved. Gait-only rigid component corrections on an anatomical leg solve restored visible femur motion while preserving the complete foot surfaces and unrelated actions. Check bone shape between keys and use physical component probes for arm/leg clearance; weight-only leg probes can miss the misassigned femurs. Preview success still requires separate client qualification.

Judge fall direction on the visible head, torso and pelvis as well as virtual spine axes. Center the collapse during release and retain a supported anatomical landing. A standing gait pose can be a poor terminal donor: the root rotation may leave a formerly horizontal tail pointing upward. Prefer a qualified source death release when it retains the intended silhouette. Inspect the body’s front and actual supports from several angles; a forward joint vector alone does not establish a prone corpse.

Per-frame shortest-arc blends from a moving source rotation into a fixed endpoint can switch branches when their relative angle crosses180 degrees. A fixed calibrated release pose and one consistent interpolation path removed such a death-phase flip while retaining the final support and equipment grip. Check relative and world limb motion between keys after any release-path change.
