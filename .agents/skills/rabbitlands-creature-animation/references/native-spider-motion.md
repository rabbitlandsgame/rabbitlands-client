# Native spider motion

Use for current imported spider models after verifying their actual geometry. The Crystal Cave and Nightweave Spider fits are user approved. Their runtime selection and checks are documented in `docs/spider-motion.md`. Nightweave has a different skeleton and asymmetric limb placement; transfer pose intent through a fresh anatomy map.

The current stylized spider sources have six large walking limbs and two smaller front palps. Labels alone can confuse the palps with forelegs. Preserve the selected source anatomy; do not invent another pair from the creature name. Separate the walking, palp and fang roles before defining support phases.

Sharp terminal blades have native point contacts, not flat paw soles. Preserve each complete blade, measure floor clearance from its actual low tip patch and reconstruct one common travel speed. Compare successive exported touchdown advances for each mirrored pair. A whole-blade centroid includes the asymmetric upper shape and can falsely report unequal steps even when the contact tips advance equally. Keep complete-blade rigidity as a separate check. For the six walking limbs, alternating tripods with a stance fraction above one half retain at least three contacts; verify the torso support point stays inside the actual contact polygon.

Fit mirrored contact lanes from the visible source endpoints rather than copying the donor's rest feet. Nightweave's middle limbs are strongly staggered in the source. Assess reach and the support polygon together: moving a rear contact backward can improve support while overextending its chain; narrowing that rear lane can recover reach. Verify the resulting advances on the exported tip surfaces.

Inspect disconnected armored pieces and the physical joints they surround. Whole pieces should rotate without changing size. Native influence names can span multiple articulated sections; record explicit geometric component ownership when needed. Keep small root plates with the body, upper pieces with the physical upper segment, lower pieces with the lower segment, and the terminal blade with its own orientation. Source gaps are not welded seams; check attachment movement visually as well as mesh-edge strain. Append measured controls in a versioned prepared source when the existing labels do not describe those pivots. Keep original geometry, skin and node/rest/bind prefixes intact when corrective fields can carry the new motion.

A death curl must release the walking contacts. Keeping every tip planted while sinking the body can drive a proximal plate through the floor despite valid IK lengths. Start from the grounded idle guide, curl through FK with the original segment lengths, then lower the rigid body onto its complete underside. A small floor-constrained rotation of a complete blade preserves its shape; vertex-level floor clamping does not. Audit early and intermediate release poses, full-body clearance, corpse support and the final hold. Curling every leg before lowering the body produced a visually suspended interval despite a passing terminal-support check. Follow the changing whole-surface support with one coherent rig displacement, smoothing the support envelope, and measure the maximum minimum surface clearance throughout collapse. Inspect the complete release pose sequence before a full export and render.

A coherent floor solve can still lift the body before it falls when long legs fold below it. Inspect body height throughout release as well as minimum clearance. On Nightweave, coordinating an upper-segment knee lift with the inward fold removed the initial rise. The deepest body support also needed an independent abdomen region: a low mouth appendage can pass a whole-body floor test while leaving the abdomen suspended. Give a central mouth piece its own coherent control when the side fang fields cannot describe it.

Inward curls can pass through the face. Check the released terminal surfaces against a head envelope in the moving body frame, and inspect the resulting paths from front, side and rear. The Crystal Cave Spider forelegs needed an outward shoulder yaw during release; its palps needed a relaxed outward flare instead of folding across the face silhouette. A collision-free convex-envelope check supplements visual inspection and does not certify every armor/body overlap.

A donor's adaptive blade-floor solver is not automatically suitable for longer claws. Nightweave produced rapid axis changes with that solver; a smooth authored blade fold plus the coherent whole-rig support solve was more stable. Keep the native segment lengths, complete blade rigidity and clearance checks when changing that strategy.

Floor-constrained leaf rotations can change rapidly around contact release. Compare the final export to the guide between keys. Increase action-specific bake density when the error exceeds the existing bound; do not weaken the shape or replay gate. Preserve rejected versions and bind all witnesses, videos and reports to the final selected output. New anatomical controls need explicit runtime attachment mapping and authoring exports need optimization before promotion.

Dense position-and-normal fields can exceed available temporary memory during corrective compression. Disk-backed per-action fields and chunked worst-vertex residual checks preserve the same basis error bound without lowering cadence or precision. Validate the reconstruction and rerun the source-bound preflight after changing that helper.
