# Native crocodilian motion

Use for current imported crocodile/alligator-style actors. Select from the current model-development roster; a related name is not evidence of an active model. Keep a new pilot as a draft until its review and promotion gates are complete.

## Inspect the actual anatomy

A glTF can contain several skeletons, including unused duplicates with repeated bone names. Inventory mesh-to-skin assignments, nonzero vertex ownership, node indices, parent links and world rest transforms before selecting a chain. Named controls can be inside the torso while a separate unused anatomical rig follows the visible limbs. Use that second rig as measured evidence only after checking it against the surface.

For the Saltmarsh Crocodile, the active semantic limb controls missed the visible shoulders, elbows, knees and wrists. A separate prepared input appends unique anatomical controls beneath the main armature while preserving the original node/rest prefix and inverse-bind prefix. Only the new control set is animated and used for bone witnesses. The old duplicate skeletons remain static. Do not construct witness dictionaries from all duplicate names; select the physical armature using the complete unique primary set.

## Make rest repairs explicit

Keep the current raw model separate from a versioned prepared input. Distinguish raw-to-prepared changes from prepared-to-export preservation. Centering an authored curled tail changes POSITION and NORMAL, even if skin weights, textures, topology and binds are exact. Report both relationships and hash both inputs. Never describe the raw geometry as unchanged after such a repose.

Find the body forward axis from anatomical shoulder and hip midpoints. For a long turned skull, full head-cloud PCA can follow broad cheeks, horns or decorative spines instead of the snout. Fit longitudinal snout-envelope cross-section midpoints and visually compare front/top views. Turn the whole skull, eyes and teeth coherently, feathering the neck attachment only.

For a curled tail, transport cross sections from a measured native curve to a centered trailing curve. Preserve cross-section offsets and normals; check the underside as well as the upper silhouette. A low tail-tip target can pull thick tail tissue below the ground even when its centerline looks reasonable. Low-hanging vegetation or detail on the tail must follow the tail too: a height-only tail ownership mask can leave it attached to the hips and drive it underground during collapse. Include the far-tail attachment region at every height. Retain original decoration unless separately asked to remove it.

## Fit the ground motion

Use an outward continuous native bend plane, not a generic mammalian pole. Transport forelimb planes through the chest and hindlimb planes through the hips. Solve measured segment lengths without stretch. Use broad whole-palm and whole-heel fields, with distinct noncollinear sole witnesses. Fit the native sole plane and mirror the lane positions without forcing every anatomical point into a symmetric rest pose.

A low four-beat walk and quicker scramble should show shoulder and thigh rotation while the armored torso remains steady. Measure actual successive left/right touchdown advances from the exported surface under one common travel speed. Matching foot excursion amplitude alone can hide a catch-up step. Check the whole planted paw for ground-speed residual, not just a single toe marker.

A braced head/body lunge is appropriate when the source has a natural gape but no clean independently moving jaw. A semantic lower-mouth bone can own only a small side patch; animating it can tear connected lips or teeth. Inspect welded connectivity and ownership first. If the mouth is connected, keep its gape in the first draft and state that an independent snap needs additional mouth separation and weighting. Adding a neutral jaw control does not establish a working jaw.

For death, lower the actual belly support surface, relax the wide feet and settle the tail. Bound head droop by full skull clearance. A grounded foot and skull do not prove the body is supported. Check all surface minima, belly support, limb lengths, tail segment lengths, continuous velocity and the final hold.

## Qualify the delivered file

Keep complete skull, torso cores and paws rigid where appropriate; regularize only the soft attachments. Bind temporal surface caches to the prepared source and processor hashes. Use the established affine corrective, convex-normal and morph-clock export checks, then re-evaluate the exported file independently. The existing binary morph-clock lattice can still lose too much authored-key precision for a new actor. Increase the binary clock resolution and recheck physical, key-replay and importer fidelity rather than relaxing the limits. Preserve the rejected candidate and its evidence.

Check fresh Blender and current game-importer surface/bone witnesses against the exact candidate hash, including off-grid times. Keep six-angle full-cadence media, pose sheets and front/rear paw close-ups. Video timing/hash checks and offline DOM tests are separate from live browser playback and human approval. Large authoring exports still need runtime optimization before promotion. Preserve the checksums and pending status of earlier family reviews when moving to the next family.
