## Low-tail collapse and foreleg loading

For a long low tail intended to rest on the floor, qualify its exposed distal support separately from head, chest and pelvis. A single native tail yaw can avoid floor intersection by lifting the whole tail high above an otherwise supported body. An independent tail pitch in the ground frame provides a useful additional fitting degree of freedom; blend it only through release and keep the final hold still. Audit every tail section length on exported keys and midpoints, then inspect the body and tail together from both rear quarters.

Complete paw clearance does not certify the rest of a thick foreleg. An early backward load combined with a lifting forepaw can fold elbow or socket tissue below the floor while the paw and bone-length checks pass. Inspect the actual worst surface vertex and its ownership. Where the muscle belongs to the leg, bring the forward drive earlier or reduce the crouch rather than incorrectly reweighting that tissue to the torso.
