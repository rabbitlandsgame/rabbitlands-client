## Visible legs and a supported kick

The native generic knee sits well in front of the visible hock. Fit the physical hip, hock and ankle from the actual mesh rather than copying the named guide chain. Include both complete toes in the contact field and use populated shin probes. An authored forward kick uses different contact rules from a two-foot bill strike. Chamber the complete striking foot, extend it forward, then recover. Check the opposite foot for surface drift and floor clearance throughout the strike. Verify the striking toes retain their shape and return to the starting pose; do not certify the kick from a forward-moving generic foot guide alone.

## Long-neck collapse and available actions

Coordinate body pitch, breast descent and the long neck fold. An excessive neck fold can put the bill on the ground while holding the body up; too little body pitch leaves the breast above the supporting upper-leg surface. Check the actual breast, intervening released-leg motion, neck, complete feet and final hold from opposing angles. A supported endpoint does not qualify the rest of the fall.
