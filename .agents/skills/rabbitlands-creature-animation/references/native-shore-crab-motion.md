# Native shore-crab fitting

Read native-crab-motion.md first, then inspect the new source. The Crystal Shore Crab is not a copy of the Bronze Tide Crab: it has eight separate three-piece outer limbs and eight actual pointed blades. Seven inner armor pieces connect those limbs to the shell; the left anterior inner piece spans two named proximal banks. Keep that shared source piece coherent.

The selected draft braces all inner armor with the carapace and articulates measured outer hinges. This is a deliberate pose constraint, not a claim that the source lacks proximal joints. Each outer chain preserves three actual rigid sections, measured segment lengths, real blade contact patches and its fixed socket relative to the braced shell. Record this choice explicitly; do not describe these limbs as the Bronze shared fore-fork.

Source leg labels do not guarantee a planted initial pose. Fit every blade's lane and floor contact, test maximum reach through the full stride and lower the shell when necessary rather than lengthening the leg or accepting a clipped IK target. Crystal's initial shell height caused reach errors, especially in its fast scuttle. A lower neutral shell resolved them while retaining eight coherent pointed contacts.

Use a stable outward bend plane for the short outer chains. Projecting the original nearly collinear lower-leg vector as the IK pole caused branch flips through stance, despite passing endpoint and length checks. A source-side outward pole removed those turns without changing the stride or relaxing continuity limits. Check full-cadence local and world turns as well as representative poses.

Use an overlapping alternating wave with one measured world advance for every stance. For contact review, sample each leg at phases inside that leg's own stance. A shared arbitrary phase can put a distant foot in swing and falsely suggest poor contact. Render individual blade close-ups from three angles, including the actual low point and surrounding rigid joints. Put each side camera on that limb's actual side of the body; a fixed positive-X camera can hide right blades behind the left legs even when its target is correct.

A bearing chosen from the main carapace alone can leave the shell suspended on adjacent braced armor. Include every body-owned rigid component when constructing the support envelope, require actual main-carapace contact in the selected bearing, and check the front and rear shell separately. Preserve failed diagnostics; do not weaken floor/support thresholds to accept the first fit. The centroid and support hull are explicit geometric proxies, not mass or dynamics.

Inspect pincer motion as well as arm translation. The two source claws can need different apparent opening because their hinge geometry differs. Check each actual fingertip's opening, positive closing gap and forward stroke relative to the body. Crystal needed a wider load and stronger closure than Bronze; a shared parameter change still required independent per-side measurements. Observe both wind-up and snap poses, not only the peak extension.

Keep the original geometry, normals, UVs, textures, topology, original node/rest prefix and inverse binds. Make rigid ownership and attachment aliases explicit prepared inputs. Qualify unsigned skin storage, exact raw/full replay, exported motion and unchanged deformation/continuity gates, then fresh independent Blender/current-Godot witnesses and full native-speed six-angle media. A numerical shape pass does not replace inspection of sockets, silhouettes, blade contact and ending support. Preserve human-review status separately; new work remains a local draft.
