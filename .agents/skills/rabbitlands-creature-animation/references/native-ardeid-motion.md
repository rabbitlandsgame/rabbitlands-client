# Native heron motion

Use this guidance for the active imported Swamp, Moor, Coralcrest and Suncrest Herons. Their private continuation records identify current candidates and review status. Heron drafts are separate from the deferred owl and corvid drafts; none implies visual acceptance or runtime promotion.

## Assess physical anatomy before transferring

Inspect the true rest surface as well as the native animated poses. Swamp Heron's generic source actions tuck its physical legs into the body, creating a misleading hovering silhouette. Its rest mesh has long bare legs and complete toes. Joint labels alone cannot distinguish a real leg from a generic fitting guide.

The four sources need separate calibration. Moor has one foot raised substantially in its sculpted rest pose. Coralcrest has duplicate left-foot branches and no named right chain. Suncrest's ankle-to-foot segment is much longer than Swamp's. A shared motion concept does not justify copying one pilot's joint indices, anatomical masks or contact targets.

## Native branches and coherent ownership

Swamp has a named spine/head chain and a separate multi-joint branch following the visible S-shaped neck. Moving only the named head parent leaves this surface branch behind. Fit both while retaining native serial segment lengths and their original rests/binds. A continuous end-constrained chain fit can follow the head attachment without adding a second skeleton.

A two-bone bend pole that becomes parallel to its target direction can suddenly switch sides. Audit dense exported poses and between-key interpolation; plausible snapshots can hide that discontinuity. Choose a stable bend frame or an authored native bend path before increasing sampling.

Treat detached crest pieces coherently with the head. Avoid a hard spatial cutoff across the connected upper neck: adjacent source rows can acquire very different frames and stretch sharply during head release. Use source topology to distinguish crest components, then a continuous transition on connected neck tissue.

Keep complete toe surfaces rigid while bare hock rows articulate. Separate torso/feather-skirt ownership from leg weights. The longest native tail feathers reach the floor even in rest; clear them about their actual tail hinge without lifting planted toes. Qualify the complete posed surface, not only the selected feet.

## Grounded gait and supported collapse

Measure successive physical touchdown advances under one ground speed, including the native left/right toe-centroid offsets. Equal control amplitudes alone do not establish equal steps. Check planted toe shape, surface normals, clearance and contact drift at the exported cadence.

Choose collapse pitch from the source torso's actual axis. Reusing an upright owl's steep pitch on the sloping heron body leaves its rump and released feet suspended. Settle the trunk, fold the long neck continuously and refit released legs after any body-floor correction. Lifting the entire skeleton after solving the feet lifts those feet off the floor again. Foot release angles must also respect the sparse ankle skin, rather than maximizing bend.

Keep native geometry, UVs, normals, textures, nodes and rest/bind data unchanged when a skin-only preparation suffices. Record skin changes explicitly and bind numerical qualification, fresh Blender import, current game-import checks and native-speed multi-angle media to the exact candidate hash. Keep visual acceptance and browser-control verification separate from those checks.


## Family transfer preparations

Use physical leg surfaces and source material colour together. A coordinate box can confuse bare shins with breast feathers or miss dark claws. Start from leg-coloured witnesses, select whole connected lower-limb fields, then inspect the transition to the torso. Source texture image zero can be a normal map; sample the material's base-colour texture explicitly.

Moor and Coralcrest need a prepared toe pose before both feet can plant. Record this as local geometry and normal preparation, not a skin-only repair. Rotate digit segments about their source joints and transport their normals with the surface; retain topology, UVs, textures and the original node/rest/bind prefix. Added physical thigh/hock/foot controls should use measured surface guides and explicit binds. Keep each complete toe surface under its foot control.

The visible S-neck can bring separate anatomical folds close together. Nearest-segment weights can jump between folds; a height-only mask can instead move breast or eye rows. Qualify a continuous anatomical field against the actual neck branch and head. A selected skin intermediate needs its own hash and exact scope; selecting a neighbouring trial by filename can reintroduce a rejected eye/neck defect.

A serial neck solve can preserve lengths yet switch between equally valid shapes during a fall or flinch. Use a consistent arch guide and segment frames, then measure maximum surface speed and exported between-key error. A small flinch may work better as a coherent lower-neck recoil than independent endpoint IK. Do not increase bake rate to hide a discontinuous solve.

Treat hanging breast plumes, rear head crests and wing panels as complete connected pieces with physical proximal anchors. A component-centroid cutoff can miss a long panel whose tip reaches the floor; inspect its bounds and attachment. Partial ownership across one crest can cause severe stretch even when the beak is coherent. Settle the trunk against the floor first, refit released feet, then rotate hanging feathers about their anchors. Feather tips must not lift the entire body into a floating death pose.


## Avoid reach-driven bouncing and stutter steps

Equal touchdown advances and planted-toe drift do not qualify the whole gait. In the first Moor/Coralcrest transfer, a per-frame leg-reach clamp lowered the entire torso when a raised foot approached its reach limit. This produced 6–15 percent body-height oscillations even though toe contact passed. Measure torso vertical excursion and velocity over the full exported cycle, and inspect recovery and landing as well as touchdown snapshots.

Choose step centres from actual leg-root and complete-toe offsets, not the named hips alone. Coralcrest's generic hips guide places targets too far behind the physical roots. Recenter each gait's common toe lane, then find a reachable torso level over the whole cycle. Use only a restrained deliberate body oscillation; do not repeatedly let the shorter leg pull the torso down. Preserve measured stride length when changing the curve normalization.

For these grounded waders, lower toe lift and a quintic world-space swing progression remove abrupt acceleration changes at recovery/plant transitions. A cubic relative-foot path can match stance velocity yet still feel abrupt. Check continuous forward toe progress under the measured ground speed, meaningful interior speed dips/repeated acceleration pulses, and foot clearance. Count velocity peaks by prominence and temporal separation; raw finite differences on imported keys can create many insignificant numerical maxima. Keep the exact backward-travel check separate from that noise filter. Visual review at native speed remains necessary.
