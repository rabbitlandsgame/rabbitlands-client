# Native scorpion motion

Use for the current imported scorpion source after checking its visible anatomy. Amethyst Scorpion is user approved and installed; see `docs/scorpion-motion.md` for its runtime selection and checks. Its rig names four leg pairs, but the first pair drives the large pincers. The visible model has three walking pairs, two large pincers, small mouth palps and a segmented dorsal tail. Preserve those roles rather than inventing an extra walking pair.

Native tip-marker bones can sit well above the visible ground contact. Place prepared tip controls at the actual low points of each complete lower-leg component. Preserve original geometry, skin and node/rest/bind prefixes. A complete lower leg can contain both the lower segment and pointed foot, with no separate ankle seam. Rotate it as one piece around the physical knee; testing every point on that whole piece for zero ground velocity would incorrectly prevent knee motion. Measure stance velocity at the actual pointed contact and check complete-piece rigidity separately.

Fit mirrored contact lanes and compare successive touchdown advances under one common travel speed. Alternating tripods need at least three walking contacts and the torso inside their actual support polygon. Keep the large pincers out of that support calculation. Inspect the knee planes and body attachment points from front, side and rear.

Separate the armored body plates, whole pincers, mouth parts and tail segments by their geometric components. Generic source influences can spread a plate across unrelated joints. Complete rigid ownership avoids changing the size of the pincers, leg sections and stinger. Keep the face and eyes coherent. A single connected pincer claw does not imply an independently opening finger.

Author the tail through length-preserving segment directions in its sagittal plane. Keep it centered over the body during locomotion and let its segments follow the same gesture rather than adding unrelated tip motion. A sting needs both a clear forward endpoint beyond the face and continuous clearance from the floor. Check the full stinger and pincers against the moving head envelope, including the return stroke. Frame the relaxed death tail as well as the raised idle tail.

A whole-body floor test can pass while the posterior armor remains suspended. Measure rear and middle armor regions independently. For Amethyst Scorpion, choosing the settling angle from the rear and middle underside levels gave better corpse support than minimizing the spread across rear, middle and head surfaces. The separate head control can relax forward while the body settles. Keep supported rigid pieces, changing support through release, a bounded initial body rise and a still terminal hold distinct from human visual approval.

Use dense, source-bound guide and final-export checks, fresh Blender and current Godot import witnesses, all seven actions from six angles, and actual tip close-ups.
