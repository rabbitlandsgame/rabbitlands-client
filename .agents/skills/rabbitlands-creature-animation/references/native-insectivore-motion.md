## Anatomy and contact

The Hedgehog quill silhouette inflates total height. Fit shoulders, elbows, hips, stance and collapse tuck to the actual limb shells and belly. Its complete detached quill components follow the torso; forward-pointing quills can overlap face bounds without belonging to the head. Keep the complete skull, ears and facial details coherent.

Shrew has a familiar small-creature hierarchy, but generic shoulder/hip guides can sit below the visible upper leg. Measure the physical socket and elbow instead of copying joint heights. Rebase guides and inverse binds explicitly while preserving the chosen rest surface. Continuous upper-leg, forearm and paw fields should follow the actual shell, with the belly and neck protected from limb leakage. Include the entire muzzle, eyes, ears and whiskers in facial ownership. A disconnected-whisker check alone will not catch neck or arm strain.

Giant Mole has a skewed torso and asymmetrically raised digging hands. Establish forward from the physical torso and head; preserve a rigid reframe of the rest surface and transform inherited root channels through the correct parent-local conjugate. Do not infer anatomical roles from names: some apparent head or ribcage names belong to finger helpers. Select the neck base as the head-motion pivot rather than a control deep inside the face.

Its native forearm has intermediate controls and finger branches attached near the elbow. Group equal-motion controls by physical segment, and include every finger/helper branch with the palm. Preserve the native three-dimensional elbow plane for a spread digging arm when a sagittal foreleg plane would twist it. Long forearms also need their own neutral forward reach; placing every palm at shoulder Z can fold the arm and stretch skin despite perfect contact numbers.

A height-only pad mask is unreliable for a raised digging hand. It can assign the low forearm to the palm. Bound each hand from its actual native distal field and spatial palm/claw surface. Include a non-collinear sole patch across the native tilted palm. Keep the full physical hind heel with the ankle, including low heel surfaces originally weighted to the calf.

## Skin and collapse checks

Weld coincident vertices before smoothing transitions. Keep rigid palm, facial and torso cores fixed. If smoothing spreads opposing limb fields or too many tail controls into a vertex, consolidate anatomical roles explicitly and preserve useful weight mass; do not silently discard influences to meet eight slots.

Inspect local edge strain and changing cross-sections as well as seam gaps. Coincident vertices can remain joined while the arm stretches badly. Verify a fixed torso-core volume and coherent facial surface independently. Keep defect-specific vertex witnesses through fresh Blender and current-client imports.

Fit a supported side collapse to the actual neck, broad forearms, belly and tail. Short limbs need a shallow tuck to remain outside the body. Use continuous release constraints and recompute the entire floor envelope after any skin or guide correction. Do not reuse an endpoint from an older prepared surface without checking the complete motion again.

Use the standard lossless export/import, off-grid skin and bone witnesses, planted-contact, serial-length, loops, held endpoints and action-switch checks.
